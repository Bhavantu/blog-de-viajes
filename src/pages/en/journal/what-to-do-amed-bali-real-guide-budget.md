**Are you planning your trip to East Bali coast?** In this real guide I'll tell you everything **to do in Amed for free**: from how to snorkel at its best black sand bays, up to updated prices of temples, maps and honest survival tips for slow travelers. Now, no more introductions, let's start the trip.

Years ago I had the dream of traveling to Bali, in Indonesia. I wanted the Bali of my dreams, that one with quiet beaches, sacred temple rituals and a real connection with nature.

Before traveling I watched videos, read reviews and researched everything necessary to "know" what's needed. And yet, when you arrive the vibe surprises you. Yes, it is overpopulated; yes, there are tourists everywhere, but there's also local people who greet you with a genuine smile. There's a Bali beyond the traditional tourist circuit; that's exactly what we set out to discover, and that's why halfway through the trip we decided to head to Amed.

Amed is a coastal strip northeast of Bali, Indonesia, surrounded by volcanic hills, rice fields, and the silhouette of Mount Agung. The name originally belonged to a small fishing village that gained popularity and eventually extended to the designation it receives today for the traditional coastal **7 villages**: Amed, Jemeluk, Bunutan, Lipah, Selang, Banyuning, and Aas. Together they form an area of approximately 14 kilometers of coastline; currently in full tourist development, the area is divided between the districts of Abang and Kubu.

## **Why Amed is a haven for slow travelers?**

For those who want to inhabit Bali, being in Amed means staying close to the beach, discovering new bays, reefs, and colorful fish every day, watching sunsets chatting with fishermen or playing soccer with kids on the sand. More than about "doing", it's about "being".

There are no large shopping centers or highways. It's a corner for those seeking to be present. For us, it was a constant source of inspiration for writing, drawing, and remote work, a place to wind down.

## **The Jukungs: The Tradition of Sailors in Bali**

One of the first things we saw in the morning in Amed is the sea dotted with colored lanterns. With the *jukungs*, wooden canoes stabilized with two lateral bamboo bases that local fishermen have been using for generations. During the tourist season, they also use them for snorkeling excursions, fishing or sunset strolls.

<div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/jukunkgs.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Jukungs en la playa de amed </span></div>

Ketut (a local fisherman and guide) told us that many times people stay without work and opt for buying a *jukung* to fish as a means of sustenance. By mid-morning, they're parked on the shore; with great care, their owners clean the motors and pull them in with ropes to accommodate them, although we've also seen how they load them onto their shoulders to keep them safe from the tide.

During the rainy season, the sea grows so much that the beach disappears beneath the waves, which crash directly against the retaining walls built to prevent water from entering their homes or enclosures.

## **Snorkeling just meters from the shore: a public gem**

The best of Amed is that its underwater garden is open to the public and free. That's to say, it's not necessary to pay for any tour to see healthy corals; they're just there, a few steps from the sand.

Similarly, if you have little time, booking **[the snorkeling experience on a boat will allow you to quickly visit the 3 main points](https://www.getyourguide.com/karangasem-l190139/baliamed-experience-of-snorkel-in-a-submarine-temple-t914585/?ranking_uuid=d8ce01cb-a94d-48a5-89f8-7361b97c2d8a&q=snorkel+amed&date_from=2026-07-19&date_to=2026-07-19&adults=1)** and enjoy navigating the classic *jukung*.

Coral reefs are one of the most inspiring things I saw on the entire island: their shapes, colors, many different fish. Although it's true that many travelers also recommend taking a getaway to Gili Islands or Nusa Penida to snorkel or dive with mantas, dolphins, and turtles, this place has its charm.

**The most recommended bays for doing snorkeling from the shore are three.** Attention, because here's the real and practical information to get straight to the beach without scams or weird stuff.

### **1. Jemeluk Bay (One of my favorites)**

It's a beautiful bay with calm and transparent waters. It's ideal to go at any time of day because it almost has no current; in the morning the visibility is usually excellent, in the afternoon it's possible but the tide drops significantly leaving the coral exposed, so entry becomes difficult.

It's famous because it houses a small underwater stone temple surrounded by coral and colorful fish that have turned the structure into their home.

* **Entry:** Free. We enter through the restaurant and bungalows "Blue Star Bungalows & Cafe"; the entrance is through an alleyway and there's space to park motorcycles. The people who work there are very friendly, and if you consume a drink or food, you can use the loungers with umbrellas.
* **Pros:** Super calm waters, ideal for beginners or kids. The temple is just a few meters from the shore.
* **Cons:** It often has quite a bit of boat traffic (nothing serious).

### **2. Lipah Beach**

It's a sandy bay a bit clearer, without many large rocks on the shore. The coral reefs here are very healthy. The beach basically splits into two sectors: one area with a lush coral garden and another a little deeper where, if you go early in the morning (with a bit of luck and wind in your favor), you can cross paths with sea turtles (in the left-hand zone).

* **Entry:** Free. We always enter and park at Viena Beach Resort (map below).
* **Pros:** Pleasant for swimming, very healthy coral garden. The tide isn't very strong. It has a beautiful view.
* **Cons:** If the tide goes down a lot it's really hard to get into the reef area (usually in the afternoons). The road leading there is pretty rough.

### **3. Japanese Shipwreck**

It's a much wilder and rockier beach located in the village of Banyuning, a bit further east. Here you'll find the remains of a small Japanese patrol boat from World War II that ran aground near the coast.

**Data:** The shipwreck is located in an area that gains depth with the waves, so visibility sometimes fluctuates according to marine currents.
**Entrance:** Free if you enter through public access points, which basically amounts to a abandoned terrain not very friendly. Another option, and one recommended in this case, is to enter through a local inn, which charges 25,000 IDR (1.39 USD) for using their facilities, parking, and entrance.
**Pros:** Fewer people than at Jemeluk, and the chance to see an historic ship covered in marine life just a few meters deep.
**Cons:** The water entry is purely rocky and the tide can be strong and push you towards the rocks; the entrance is viable but slow and requires caution. I wouldn't recommend it for children personally.

## **The Mecca of Scuba Diving and Freediving**

It's impossible to talk about Amed without mentioning that it's one of the most outstanding diving destinations globally, so even though we don't practice it, we'll leave a brief section on a very significant topic in the area.

* **Freediving (Apnea):** Due to the coastline's sudden depth gain and lack of strong currents in many bays, Amed is an important point for apnea practice. There are many schools where you're taught to breathe and descend several meters while holding your breath. It's a very interesting physical practice as it requires a specific mental and bodily state of presence and relaxation (and training). In fact, there are centers (such as Apneista Bali) that combine it with yoga practice.
* **Scuba diving:** If you ever thought about getting certified (*Discover Scuba Diving*) or certifying your first level (*Open Water*), this is an ideal place to do so. The calm waters and affordable prices with good recommendations. We left it pending and finished it in Nusa Penida, but honestly, we really wanted to. One of the schools that recommends it most is **Amed White Sand Divers**..
* **The USAT Liberty in Tulamben:** Just 15 minutes by motorbike north of Amed lies Tulamben. There lies the *USAT Liberty*, a US military transport ship wrecked and then pushed into the sea by the eruption of Mount Agung in 1963. It's considered one of the most accessible wrecks in the world, suitable for both beginners (it's at shallow depth) and advanced divers.

## **Tourist Circuits: Points of Interest**

During our 15 days in Amed, we mostly focused on living day-to-day, snorkeling, and working. But we also explored the surrounding areas and tourist attractions, historical sites, and incredible landscapes really close by. Here's all the information on things to do in Amed below.

### **Sweet Occupation: a park in the mountains with views of Mount Agung**

It's a set of viewpoints located on the mountain with really incredible views. The access road by motorcycle is steep, paved, and not suitable for heart patients (but possible) or you can hire a local driver to go there (it makes perfect sense).

* **Entrance price:** IDR 50,000 (approximately USD 3). Includes parking and the shuttle bus that takes you to the summit.

### **Pure Lempuyang (The Gates of Heaven)**

It's a complex of beautiful temples that has been very popularized by famous traveler photos taken between two classic-style pillars called "The Gates to Heaven" with the volcano in the background and a reflection of water from a lake that doesn't exist; instead, someone places a small mirror underneath their smartphone camera to create this effect (which looks quite spectacular). The lines to take that photo can last anywhere from 1:30 to 4 hours. The more you're near sunrise, the less people you'll encounter. The general admission costs IDR 100,000 (around USD 6) and the mandatory shuttle bus service for round-trip travel up to the temple costs an additional IDR 50,000 (around USD 3).

If you keep going up, you'll find yourself in a completely different experience: other temples, fewer tourists, with fog and mountain views. You may even cross paths with local people making their offerings.

### **Ujung Water Palace (Sukasada Water Palace)**

This palace is a complex of stunning ponds and historic bridges known as Taman Ujung. It's located in the village of Tumbu and honestly was a real gem that surprised us. What's more, it was built in 1909 by King Anak Agung Anglurah of Karangasem, blending Balinese, European, and Chinese architecture in an astonishing way. Currently, it operates as a tourist center, well-maintained and cared for, and admission costs 100,000 IDR (around USD 6) and you can combine it with the two next points on the same day.

### **The Royal Water Palace: Tirta Gangga**

An ancient royal palace surrounded by Balinese gardens and pools of water decorated with traditional statues. The main attraction is walking on the stone paths that float on the water, feeding giant koi carp that live there. It's a very visual and relaxing stroll to do at half past noon.

* **Entrance cost:** 100,000 IDR (around $6).
* **Parking:** 2,000 IDR for motorcycles and 5,000 IDR for cars (less than $0.50).

## **Hiking near volcanic areas around Amed**

The silhouettes of the volcanoes dominate the entire landscape and, although we haven't climbed them yet, we're gathering the key information that every adventurous traveler needs before embarking on a good trekking experience.

### **Mount Batur: Classic Sunrise Over the Caldera**

For those seeking a relatively accessible hike but with incredible views, Monte Batur (1717 meters) is the top choice on the island. It's an active volcano whose last significant eruption occurred in 2000. Located in the Kintaman area. The majority of excursions begin at 3:00 or 4:00 am to reach the summit and watch the sunrise, mandatory guide required.

* **Experience Data:** It's a medium-difficulty trekking. It's not a technical trail, but it is demanding due to the constant slope over volcanic soil and loose ash. The starting level is approximately 1000m above sea level and the ascent is 600-700m of positive slope. It takes between 1.5 and 2 hours to reach the summit.
* **The Reward:** A panoramic view of Lake Batur, Mount Abang, and on clear days, the distant silhouette of Mount Rinjani in neighboring Lombok Island, surrounded by a dense mantle of low clouds.
* [You can book your tour walking or with jeep ascent here](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/bali-l347/bali-monte-batur-sunrise-trek-con-entrada-t401391?ranking_uuid=72e57dfc-5a0e-44eb-8860-ad7db6dca002&date_from=2026-08-21)

### **Mount Agung: active volcano and otherworldly views**

The Mount Batur is the highest point on the island and the most sacred volcano for the Balinese, who consider it the "navel of the world". Climbing it involves a greater challenge since

* **The Experience:** It's a high-difficulty trekking that is demanding and technical since the summit is at around 10,342 meters. There are two main routes: one that starts from Pura Pasar Agung temple (which reaches the crater rim at around 9,286 meters and takes around 3-4 hours to ascend). The complete route from Besakih's mother temple (which ascends to the real summit at 10,342 meters, requiring between 5 and 7 hours of continuous ascent over steep rock). You start walking near 11:00 pm the previous day.
* **Considerations:** It requires physical fitness preparation to face the challenge and suitable footwear. Additionally, since it's a sacred site, there are periods throughout the year when access is prohibited due to religious ceremonies at the volcano temples.

* [You can book the private tour with local guides here](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/karangasem-l190139/ruta-al-amanecer-por-el-monte-agung-con-cafe-y-miel-en-una-casa-local-t1211975?ranking_uuid=b26881a4-20bb-44e8-bcf2-e0ebb967afce&q=Monte+Agung&date_from=2026-08-20)

You'll ascend via one of three available trails and then visit the guide's home in the area where you're invited with a Balinese coffee and told about the sustainable honey collection process, following traditional methods from the region. Honestly, this proposal seems like a total hit.

## **Magical Sunsets: Where to Watch the Sunset in Marmaris?**

The sunset in Amed is a daily ritual. Being on the eastern coast, the sun hides behind the contour of the Agung volcano, gifting unforgettable postcards. Here are three places where you can take something and watch the sun fall. PS: the beach is always a good plan.

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/atardecer-amed-beach-vistas-al-mont-batur-.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Atardecer en la playa de amed (amed beach)</span></div>

* **Amed Beach (The Local and Free Option):** keep it simple, sit on the rocks or black sand. With the sound of waves, the silhouette of the mountain, and the sun setting directly behind the mountain, it's a free show.
* Beach Bars: a plan that includes a fresh natural juice or something to drink for approximately 30,000 IDR around (1.67 USD) (1.46 €) at many places with live music, and puffing on the beach with a view of the sea.
* **Amed Sunset Point (The Pool Experience):** It's a very popular lookout point with an infinity pool that points directly to Jemeluk Bay and the volcano. They have live music and sometimes traditional shows (like fire dancing).

*Cost:*
They charge a minimum fee (*minimum spend*) of **IDR 125,000 per person** (around USD 8) that you pay upon entry and can be used for drinks or food.

* **The Local Pub Across the Street (Our Hidden Gem):** If you'd like to skip the minimum spend and more touristy atmosphere of Sunset Point, just across the street is a local warung. They have the same view, welcome you with all the love, and charge you a fraction of the price for a Bintang or the island classic: **Balinese arak.** It's a traditional drink, distilled from palm or rice similar to white rum that combines with different fruits and flavors.

## **Everything You Need to Know Before You Go: Practical Details**

**Transport:** The motorbike is quite ideal for those who drive with audacity. In Amed, it allows you to move from bay to bay with freedom, but that's it: the routes have pronounced curves and steep inclines, and the road isn't always in optimal conditions. If you don't feel 100% secure driving a scooter, there's no need to risk your health. At the same homestays, you can hire very predisposed drivers. Traffic is more friendly than in Ubud or Canggu.

⚠️ **Effective / ATMs:** The cash machines (ATM) in the area frequently run out of cash. We recommend withdrawing in Amed in advance.

**Frequently Asked Questions**

* **How is the beach in Amed?** It's characterized by having volcanic black sand and many rocks. The water is transparent and the current isn't strong. In the bays the wind is relatively calm.
* **Can you walk along the beach?** Yes, but with nuances: the coarse sand and rocks can be a massage or a nuisance, but it resolves easily with footwear.
* In the morning the tide is quite high and there are the *jukungs* (classic Bali boats); so you can walk but you have to get closer to the water, avoid the boats, etc.
* The afternoon is the ideal moment since the sunset is beautiful, many restaurants have live music and the tide is lower.
