## What to do in Ubud, Bali, Indonesia?

#Ubud Guide
Ubud combines authentic Balinese tradition with an infinite tourist proposal. In this complete guide, you'll find activities of interest to do in Ubud, costs, and recommendations based on what we most enjoyed during our stay.

* ## Introduction

Since we arrived in Ubud, every aspect of its aura has been delighting us: from its incredible constructions that seem like temples everywhere, to its *penjors*, bamboo handicrafts that hang outside the houses this time of year to celebrate Kuningan Day, a local ritual in Balinese Hindu tradition. The attentive and friendly people smiled down the streets, and in the small alleys, children pedaled their bicycles back and forth between laughter and shouts.

From the start, it seemed to us that there was a different aura here: yes, it's touristy, but also more tranquil. There's a lot of genuinely local and friendly people who look you in the eye and stop on the street to ask: *How are you?* That's how Ubud taught us about Balinese culture and became our base for a 10-day period.

If you want to delve into the culture, you can read [The Soul of Bali: Rituals, Family, and the Secret of Balinese Happiness](https://blogcito.com/diario/2026-06-23-el-alma-de-bali-rituales-la-familia-y-el-secreto-de-la-felicidad-balinesa/)

* ## Data about Ubud:

Ubud, located between Bali's central mountains, is an area that perfectly combines tourism and local culture. The center is filled with tourist-oriented restaurants and experiences, but also many *warungs* (small family businesses) and *guest houses* (houses with rooms managed by the local population, always eager to share their practices and culture).

*Activities in Ubud are almost endless, and drivers willing to take you on a tour of the various points as well. The truth is that [contracting a trusted driver and passing through several points in one day with a local guide is a great option, especially if you have a reduced travel time.*](https://www.booking.com/attractions/id/prlacetrgqo0-ubud-explorer-private-day-tour.es.html?aid=2439984&label=product-attractions-KUOw4lVyFHxONkKhk1ijJQS754810308874%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-1967745930671%3Alp9072522%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Acid22520705213%3Aagid178703962946&source=searchresults-product-card&ufi=-2701757&date=2026-07-08&timeslot=ATS-PRlaCEtrgQo0-202607080700-nullnull&ticket_type=OF6FMaTt5Smw)

## 1. The Rice Terraces and the Subak System

One of my must-haves was to visit the famous rice terraces in Bali that are known for it. What surprised me pleasantly is that, just by stepping out of Ubud's city center, the plantations can be found everywhere, leaving a green and open landscape to enjoy breathtaking sunsets or starry nights with incredible views.

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/dsc_0628.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Campos de arroz Tegalalang Rice Terrace </span></div>

There are two major terraces declared international heritage thanks to their irrigation system called **Subak**. This traditional water management system dates back to the 9th century and reflects the philosophy of *Tri Hita Karana*, which seeks harmony between the realm of spirits, human society, and nature. It is ancestral agricultural engineering and represents a spiritual and cooperative practice that unites the entire community in the care of resources.

**Real Data about the Terraces and My Personal Reflection:** both terraces are enabled for tourism, but they still serve as work zones for local farmers; therefore, it's important to respect the trails and workers. In fact, personally, I was quite impacted by walking and encountering people working in the sun's rays, some of them very old asking for alms. The image was quite strong and left me with a thorn inviting reflection on the different realities we inhabit and how important it is to feel grateful for this vital experience. Some greeted kindly, others invited you to take photos with typical attire in exchange for a collaboration, and many have small stalls where you can sit and enjoy the views while sipping a cold coconut.

### Famous Tegalalang Rice Terraces

It's the most popular and touristy. Its views are equally beautiful. It can be found about 15-20 minutes north of Ubud's city center.

* **The trail and access:** The trekking is quite long if you decide to do it complete, but you can opt for shorter sections since it has multiple entrances and exits. The property has free parking.
* **Entry costs:** It costs around **$25,000 IDR** (official government payment of Indonesia where they give you a wristband with access).
* **Donations along the way:** At some points on the route there are "donation stands" (it's usually suggested to leave around 10.000 IDR) to collaborate with the maintenance of the bridges and paths managed and repaired by local farmers.
* **When to go:** The views are spectacular and you can do the trail anytime throughout the year. There is a marked trail and different entrances. Additionally, tourist activities in this area are multiple, such as famous swings (*swings*) and hanging bicycles, whose prices range from 150,000 to 350,000 IDR.

### Jatiluwih Rice Terraces (UNESCO's Jewel)

This is the one that's way more towards the north and it's a lot less touristy. It's, in fact, a formally declared Cultural Landscape by UNESCO.

* **Why Go:** It's a protected area and much larger. You can walk for long periods without encountering many tourists. The air is fresher because it's at higher elevation, although the walking paths are a bit more "off-road".
* **Costs:** The entrance fee has an approximate value of **40,000 IDR** (dedicated to maintenance and preservation of this massive site).
* **How to Get There:** It's about 60-90 minutes from Ubud. The return trip is spectacular because it goes through very picturesque traditional villages. There are many day trips that depart from central Ubud and include local guides with deep knowledge of the area.

## 4. The experience of coffee and the controversial Kopi Luwak: the world's most expensive coffee

The experience of having a coffee tasting is a classic in Bali. There are hundreds of local plantations where you're invited to drop by, learn the traditional process of roasting and treating the grains, and even get treated to a free sampling of flavored teas and coffees. It's nice to reciprocate the hospitality by buying something from their store or leaving a small contribution, but it's not "required".

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-izquierda"><img src="/img/diario/muestra-de-te-y-café-ubud.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Degustación de té y café local de Bali indonesia </span></div>

Many of these plantations have the famous experience of the coffee "world's most expensive" (*Kopi Luwak*). This coffee is extracted from beans that went through the digestive process of the *luwak* (civet), a small mammal of the viverrid family that chooses the sweetest and ripest coffee fruit. As they pass through its intestinal tract and are excreted, enzymes modify the grain proteins, removing bitterness. The collectors manually gather the beans, thoroughly clean them, and then undergo the toasting process.

**The Ethical Dilemma of Luwaks:** The question regarding the care and living conditions of these animals remains a great mystery to me. Although they may say that the grains are wild in origin, the high tourist demand generated that many plantations keep the luwaks in very tight cages to force them to only eat coffee. I think a possible ethical path, if you decide to try it, is to thoroughly research the place and avoid promoting sites where you see animals in captivity for exhibition purposes.

Despite this, there are places like "Lokasari Agrowisata" that offer an interesting **cultural**, **artistic**, and **ethical** experience in their coffee plantation fields, organic herbal tea production, and honey production.

## 5. Temples: Rules of Respect and the Rite of Purification

The population of neighboring Java Island often calls Bali "the island of a thousand temples," and it's no joke: in each village, there is at least one public temple, plus family shrines that each house has in its yard.

### Rules for entering temples in Bali:

Temples are spectacular and sacred places for the local population, so there are a couple of general rules for coexistence that need to be followed:

* **Appropriate Attire:** Always wear clothing that covers your knees and shoulders. A *sarong* (traditional fabric worn like a skirt) is mandatory. Many temples lend or rent them.
* **Respect the Environment:** Maintain silence and respect the space as it is: a place of prayer and active spiritual connection.
* **The Menstruation Rule:** There are signs at the entrances restricting women's entry during menstruation. Which surprised me quite a bit, but reading the explanation links it to the concept of *sebel* in Balinese Hindu cosmology, understood as a state of spiritual vulnerability or temporary ritual impurity related to blood. It presents itself as a rule for energetic balance and sacred space purity that is respected rigorously.

## The Rite of Purification (Melukat)

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/ritual-espiritual.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Ritual de purificación Balines en piscinas sagradas, Ubud </span></div>

Many of the temples are centered around purification through water, a sacred healing ritual that is performed in various pools with springs depending on the spiritual purpose of each jet of water.

To perform this ritual in a respectful and correct manner, it is necessary to have a local guide accompany you, instruct you on the steps, and help you make the offerings. I know there are many controversies and debates online about this topic and the truth is that for those of us who genuinely want to connect with this faith practice, it's not pleasant to be haggling over money or worried about potential scams. That's why I'm leaving you the **information** that in my case was one of the most **important aspects of the trip:**

* **General Admission to the Temple:** The general admission fee (e.g., at the famous Tirta Empul temple) is around **$5.75 USD** per adult. Payment is made at the official ticket booth. With your admission, you'll be provided with a basic Sarong to explore the complex, but it's your responsibility to ensure your shoulders are covered. **NO need to buy a sarong from vendors at the entrance.** If sellers insist and you don't want to buy, simply smile and continue on towards the official ticket booth.
* **Local Guide:** A guide who explains the history, mysticism of the place, and accompanies you step-by-step in the pool is necessary to avoid disrespect (not to mention we're not clear if the ritual can be done without a guide). [You can contact certified guides in advance through internet](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/bali-l347/bali-visita-al-templo-gunung-kawi-con-ritual-de-purificacion-t1141894/?ranking_uuid=12e8c29b-2205-4eb2-ad7e-128b215afda8&q=ritual+de+purificacion&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08) or hire one at the temple entrance. Our advice for this second option is to clearly agree on their fees before starting (as we've learned from experience). The guide's fee typically ranges around **$15 USD** per person.
* **The Purification Ritual (Additional Costs):**

* **Ritual Attire:** It is mandatory to enter the water with a special purification sarong (you cannot use the same dry sarong you walk around the temple in). The rental cost is approximately **15,000 IDR**.
  * **Lockers (Casilleros):** To leave your dry belongings while you're in the water, the rental cost is between **10,000 to 15,000 IDR** per person.
  * **Offerings (Canang Sari):** These are small baskets of palm leaves with flowers and incense that are presented before entering the water. They have a cost of approximately **10,000 to 20,000 IDR** (usually facilitated by the guide).
* **Estimated Total Experience Cost:** Approximately between **200,000 to 250,000 IDR** (around 13-16 USD) for performing a complete and respectful guided purification ceremony, including entries, rentals, and offerings.

A reasonable option that seems to us after our experience, to avoid breaking the mystique of the moment, is to book in advance a [tour that includes everything](https://www.getyourguide.com/en-us/bali/l347/bali-visit-to-gunung-kawi-temple-with-purification-ritual-t1141894/?ranking_uuid=12e8c29b-2205-4eb2-ad7e-128b215afda8&q=ritual+of+purification&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08).

We find interesting [this tour that includes the ritual, entrance fees, additional costs, and you can also get to know Jatiluwih's (less touristy) rice terraces](https://www.getyourguide.com/en-us/jatiluwih/l88105/bali-tour-of-jatiluwih-rice-terraces-and-water-purification-t1327978/?ranking_uuid=7a0f57c5-f655-4f07-ba90-7c8c509de41d&q=Rice+Terrace+of+Jatiluwih&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08).

## 6. Traditional Dance: The Power of Kecak and the Elegance of Legong

"The 'Fire Dance' (*Kecak*) is impressive due to its frenetic rhythm, but the women's dances (*Legong*) possess elegance and control of bodily movements. Originally, these dances were performed in royal courts in front of nobility to recount founding myths."

Both take place in sacred temples: the *Legong* dance, which is performed daily at the palace and changes according to the day, but everything takes place near Ubud central. The Fire Dance *Kecak* also takes place there, changing depending on the day. Both performances start at 07:30 pm, so it's recommended to arrive 30 minutes earlier to get a good spot. Tickets can be purchased online, at the entrance, or from authorized street vendors who kindly hand you a brochure and answer all your questions. The ticket costs approximately 100,000 IDR, which is around $5.60 USD or €4.90. Refreshments are also sold at the entrance.

On this occasion, we opted for the famous **Kecak Dance** ([https://www.getyourguide.com/ubud-l32246/bali-entradas-para-el-espectaculo-de-danzas-kecak-y-del-fuego-de-ubud-t761650/?ranking_uuid=a25889a5-e5aa-4cf1-bb86-4ca8e92b23a2&q=ubud+Danza+Kecak%3A+la+danza+del+fuego&adults=1]), an impressive cultural show that features **over 70 actors on stage**.

## [Fire Dance: The Dance of Fire](https://www.getyourguide.com/ubud-l32246/bali-entradas-para-el-espectaculo-de-danzas-kecak-y-del-fuego-de-ubud-t761650/?ranking_uuid=a25889a5-e5aa-4cf1-bb86-4ca8e92b23a2&q=ubud+Fire+Dance%3A+The+Dance+of+Fire&adults=1)

The fascinating thing about this production is that it doesn't use any musical instruments as background. The sound, atmosphere, and rhythm are created exclusively by a chorus of over 70 men seated in concentric circles, who modulate their voices imitating different sounds and enter into a state of sonic trance throughout the performance (this is a personal reflection due to the peculiar way they expel air through their throats) and the constant rhythm sustains the show.

At the beginning, the dance tells a fragment of the Hindu epic *Ramayana*: the love story of Prince Rama and Princess Sita, the kidnapping of the latter by the evil multi-headed demon Ravana, and the subsequent mystical battle where the brave monkey army led by the simian god Hanuman intervenes. At the end (during the last 10 or 15 minutes of the performance), the fire dance unfolds, where a barefoot dancer in a state of mystical trance steps on and scatters lit coconut embers with their own feet.

## 7. Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary: The sacred home of monkeys

Located at the end of the bustling Monkey Forest Road, this sacred forest is an ecological and spiritual reserve managed by the local community of Padangtegal.

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/UBUD30.webp" alt="Monos en el Bosque sagrado de los monos, macacos bebes en el Monkey forest"></span> class="img-caption">Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary</span></div>

This sanctuary is governed by the concept of *Tri Hita Karana* (harmony between humans, nature, and God), preserving three active sacred temples from the 14th century that can be seen from the outside but not entered. It's a flat, easy-to-walk path, an interesting local experience suitable for all ages. Additionally, the path has many trees so the heat isn't terrible, making it a more enjoyable experience.

**Entrance Costs:** The entrance fee is **$130,000 IDR** for international adult visitors and **$100,000 IDR** for children (approximately $7.27 USD or €6.37 for adults and $5.60 USD or €4.90 for children). The park is open every day from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM and has self-service kiosks that accept cards, or tickets can be purchased online. There are also guides available who can accompany you and explain the tour of this or other points of interest.

**Strict Safety Rules:** Although the long-tailed macaques are very gentle, they are still wild animals. For your safety and theirs, the center recommends:

* Don't enter with loose plastic bags or bottles.
* Avoid getting too close, especially to those with their babies (although we actually saw a lot of people doing this above all, literally).
* Don't stare at them directly: since in their body language, direct eye contact is a sign of aggression.
* Don't hide food in your backpack and be cautious with personal items like glasses or shiny objects, as they are very curious and trusting.

## 8. Ubud Palace (Puri Saren Agung)

<div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/palacio-real-ubud-bali.jpeg" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Palacio real Ubud durante el dia</span></div>

Located at the corner of Jalan Raya Ubud and Jalan Monkey Forest, facing the traditional art market, the Ubud Palace is the geographical and historical epicenter of the city. Built during the reign of Ida Tjokorda Putu Kandel (1800-1823), it still serves as an official residence for the royal family of Ubud, so only its public exterior patio and intricately carved stone gates decorated with golden leaves can be explored.

* **Day visit:** Entry is completely **free** during the day (from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM). It takes about 10 or 15 minutes to get around; I think it's a practical stop since it's in the city center and allows you to admire up close the marvelous classical Balinese palace architecture. Plus, inside there's a restaurant.
* **Night shows:** Every night, from 7:00 PM onwards, the main courtyard transforms into an illuminated stage lit by torches for traditional Balinese women's dance *Legong*.

*Editor's Note: Dear reader, please note that prices and regulations in Bali fluctuate according to season and updates from local administrations of each regency. It is recommended to always carry cash in Indonesian rupiah (IDR), as many temples and waterfalls do not accept credit cards.*

## Frequently Asked Questions

* #### What is the best time to visit Ubud?

The best time is during the dry season, which runs from **April to October**. During these months there are fewer rains, the paths of waterfalls and rice fields are in better condition, and the climate is ideal for exploring.

**Is it safe to explore Ubud and its surroundings?**

Yes, Ubud is an extremely safe destination for travelers, families, and solo travelers. Just take basic common-sense precautions, such as wearing a helmet when renting a scooter, being cautious with valuable items at the Monkey Forest, and booking guided tours with official guides. Special caution should be taken when walking on the street since outside of the city center, there are usually no sidewalks to walk on; in general, drivers are attentive, but it's recommended to walk during the day, carefully and in a group.

* #### How to Get Around in Ubud?

If you have experience, renting a **scooter** is the fastest and most affordable way (between 50,000 and 80,000 IDR per day). If you prefer to avoid intense traffic in the city center or don't drive a motorcycle, you can hire a taxi or **hire a private driver for the day** to explore waterfall routes and temples or use apps like **Gojek/Grab** for short trips.

* #### How much does the entrance fee to Monkey Forest Ubud cost in 2026?

The general entry fee for international adults costs **$130.000 IDR** (approx. $7.20 USD / €6.30) and for children **$100.000 IDR** (approx. $5.50 USD / €4.90). Tickets can be purchased directly at self-service machines or online.
