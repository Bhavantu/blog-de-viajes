Ubud combines authentic Balinese tradition with an endless range of tourist offerings. In this complete guide you'll find activities worth doing in Ubud, costs and recommendations based on what we enjoyed most during our stay.

## Introduction

Ever since we arrived in Ubud, every part of its aura began to delight us: from its incredible buildings that look like temples everywhere, to its *penjors*, bamboo crafts that hang outside the houses at this time of year to celebrate Kuningan Day, a local ritual of the Balinese Hindu tradition. The kind, attentive people smiled in the streets and, in the small alleys, children rode their bikes back and forth amid laughter and shouts.

From the start it seemed to us that there was a different aura here: yes, it's touristy, but also calmer. There are lots of genuinely local, friendly people who look you in the eye and stop in the street to ask you: *how are you?* That's how Ubud taught us about Balinese culture and became our base for 10 days.

## Facts about Ubud

Ubud, which sits among Bali's central mountains, is an area that blends the touristy and the local perfectly. The center is full of restaurants and experiences geared toward tourism, but you'll also find lots of *warungs* (small family-run businesses) and *guest houses* (homes with rooms run by the local population, always ready to share their practices and culture).

Activities in Ubud are almost endless, and so are the drivers willing to show you a tour of the different spots. The truth is that [hiring a trusted driver and visiting several spots in one day with a local guide is a great option, especially if you have limited travel time.](https://www.booking.com/attractions/id/prlacetrgqo0-ubud-explorer-private-day-tour.es.html?aid=2439984&label=product-attractions-KUOw4lVyFHxONkKhk1ijJQS754810308874%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-1967745930671%3Alp9072522%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Acid22520705213%3Aagid178703962946&source=searchresults-product-card&ufi=-2701757&date=2026-07-08&timeslot=ATS-PRlaCEtrgQo0-202607080700-nullnull&ticket_type=OF6FMaTt5Smw)

## What to do in Ubud?

## 1. The rice terraces and the Subak system

One of the must-dos for me was seeing the famous rice terraces Bali is known for. What pleasantly surprised me is that, just by leaving the center of Ubud, the plantations are everywhere, leaving the green, open landscape to watch the sunset or the stars with incredible views.

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/dsc_0628.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Rice fields at Tegalalang Rice Terrace</span></div>

There are two large terraces, and one of them, Jatiluwih, is part of UNESCO heritage thanks to its irrigation system called **Subak**. This traditional water management system dates back to the 9th century and reflects the philosophy of *Tri Hita Karana*, which seeks harmony between the spirit realm, the human world and nature. It is ancestral agricultural engineering and represents a spiritual, cooperative practice that unites the whole community in caring for resources.

**Real facts about the terraces and my personal reflection:** both terraces are open to tourism, but they remain working areas for local farmers; therefore, it is important to respect the paths and the workers. In fact, personally, it hit me quite hard to be walking and run into people working under the blazing sun, some of them quite elderly, asking for alms. The image was quite strong and left me with a thorn, inviting reflection on the different realities we inhabit and how important it is to feel grateful for this life experience. Some of them greeted kindly, others invited you to take photos in traditional dress in exchange for a contribution, and many also have small stalls where you can sit to enjoy the views and have a cold coconut.

### Tegalalang Rice Terrace (The famous one)

It is the most popular and touristy. Its views are just as beautiful. It is about 15-20 minutes north of central Ubud.

* **The path and access:** The trek is quite long if you decide to do all of it, but you can opt for short stretches since it has multiple entrances and exits. The site has free parking.
* **Entrance costs:** It costs about **25,000 IDR** (official payment to the Indonesian government, where they give you an access wristband).
* **Donations along the way:** At some points along the route there are local "donation stalls" (leaving about 10,000 IDR is usually suggested) to help maintain the bridges and paths that the farmers themselves manage and repair.
* **When to go:** The views are spectacular and you can do the route all year round. There is a marked trail and different entrances. In addition, there are many tourist activities in this area, like the famous swings and hanging bikes, whose prices range between 150,000 and 350,000 IDR.

[You can book your visit with a guide and photos in the rice fields here](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/bali-l347/ubud-entrada-a-la-terraza-de-arroz-y-columpio-de-tegallalang-con-guia-t456886/?ranking_uuid=7604d510-5b5f-40f0-9755-6444cd1b50c4)

### Jatiluwih Rice Terrace (The UNESCO jewel)

This is the one farthest north and it is far less touristy. It is, in fact, a Cultural Landscape formally declared by UNESCO.

* **Why go:** It's a protected area and much larger. You can walk for long stretches without running into so many tourists. The air is cooler because it sits at a higher altitude, although the walking paths are a bit more "off-road".
* **Costs:** Entrance is approximately **40,000 IDR** (used for the maintenance and preservation of this giant site).
* **How to get there:** It's about 60-90 minutes from Ubud. The outbound trip is spectacular because it crosses very picturesque traditional villages. There are many day excursions that leave from central Ubud and include local guides with deep knowledge of the area.

## 2. Waterfalls: A refuge from Ubud's heat

<div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/cascada-ulu-petanu-ubud.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Ulu Petanu waterfall - Ubud Bali</span></div>

There are plenty of waterfall options around Ubud and they're an excellent spot to cool off after a walk in the sun. The time you go is always a key factor regarding the number of tourists, but from my point of view they're always enjoyable. That said: although they usually have maintenance and access steps, keep in mind that after rain there's usually quite a bit of mud and slippery areas (not serious, just be careful).

Although there are dozens of options, here are the three that the locals we met along the way recommended to us most:

1. **[Kanto Lampo:](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/ubud-l32246/ubud-cascadas-y-templo-con-terraza-de-arroz-en-tour-en-grupo-reducido-t484232/?ranking_uuid=13d6c0db-8eca-42aa-a5ca-99810cda8039&q=tibumana+waterfall&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08)** About 30 minutes from Ubud. It's super scenic because the water falls over a wall of stepped rocks. The access has quite a few steps and it's usually crowded, but it's very beautiful. The entrance fee is 25,000 IDR approx 1.39 USD or 1.22 euros.  
2. **[Tibumana waterfall](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/ubud-l32246/ubud-cascadas-y-templo-con-terraza-de-arroz-en-tour-en-grupo-reducido-t484232/?ranking_uuid=13d6c0db-8eca-42aa-a5ca-99810cda8039&q=tibumana+waterfall&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08):** It's a small waterfall surrounded by dense jungle with a perfect, straight drop of water that forms a natural pool ideal for swimming and taking incredible photos. The entrance has steps and a small temple at the front. Although we didn't visit it, it was a waterfall that stayed on our list from the local recommendations. Tickets cost 15,000 IDR, which is approximately 0.84 USD or 0.74 euros.
3. **[Ulu Petanu waterfall:](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/ubud-l32246/tour-por-ubud-con-guia-opcional-en-ingles-espanol-e-italiano-t895337/?ranking_uuid=22ec2f2f-5cf6-40d7-9f4d-6a2f07016c88&q=Cascada+Ulu+Petanu&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08)** A hidden gem 40 minutes from central Ubud. It's a jungle area with pools used for religious ceremonies at the entrance; you have to go down 90 steps, but it's a fairly peaceful place with free parking. It has a main waterfall where you can bathe, another small and more secluded one where some locals go to do small daily cleansing ceremonies, and a historic cave called Tampaksiring of approximately 200 flat meters; the story goes that it was dug by hand as part of the rice-field irrigation system in ancient times. Entrance to this cave has a significant cost of 10,000 IDR, and entrance to the waterfall 30,000 IDR. The total cost of both tickets is around 2.23 USD or 1.95 euros.

## 4. The coffee experience and the controversial Kopi Luwak: the world's most expensive coffee

Doing a coffee tasting is a Bali classic. There are hundreds of local plantations where they invite you in, show you the traditional roasting and bean-processing method, and even treat you to a free tasting of flavored teas and coffees. It's nice to repay the hospitality by buying something in their shop or leaving a small contribution, but it's not "mandatory".

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-izquierda"><img src="/img/diario/muestra-de-te-y-café-ubud.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Tasting of local tea and coffee in Bali, Indonesia</span></div>

Many of these plantations offer the famous "world's most expensive" coffee experience (*Kopi Luwak*). This coffee is extracted from beans that went through the digestive process of the *luwak* (the civet), a small mammal of the viverrid family that chooses the sweetest and ripest coffee cherries. As they pass through its intestinal tract and are excreted, enzymes modify the proteins in the bean, removing its bitterness. Collectors gather the beans by hand, clean them thoroughly and then roast them.

**The ethical dilemma of the luwaks:** The question of the care and living conditions of these animals remains a big mystery to me. Although they say the beans are of wild origin, high tourist demand has led many plantations to keep luwaks in very cramped cages to force them to eat only coffee. I think a possible ethical path, if you decide to try it, is to research the place well and avoid supporting sites where you see animals in captivity for display purposes.

Even so, there are places like "Lokasari Agrowisata" that offer an interesting [cultural, artisanal and ethical experience in their coffee plantation fields, with organic herbal tea and honey production.](https://www.getyourguide.com/sidemen-village-l92066/lokasari-tour-por-una-plantacion-de-cafe-te-de-hierbas-y-miel-pura-t1040405?ranking_uuid=36029e36-77dd-4afa-b226-b241f7e7449b&q=Tours+sobre+caf%C3%A9,+Bali&date_from=2026-07-10) *([This tour does not include transfers](https://www.getyourguide.com/sidemen-village-l92066/lokasari-tour-por-una-plantacion-de-cafe-te-de-hierbas-y-miel-pura-t1040405?ranking_uuid=36029e36-77dd-4afa-b226-b241f7e7449b&q=Tours+sobre+caf%C3%A9,+Bali&date_from=2026-07-10))*

## 5. Temples: Rules of respect and the Purification Ritual

The people of the neighboring island of Java often call Bali "the island of a thousand temples", and it's no joke: every village has at least one public temple, plus the family shrines that every house has in its courtyard.

### Rules for entering temples in Bali:

Temples are spectacular places and sacred to the local population, so there are a couple of general rules of conduct that must be followed:

* **Appropriate clothing:** Always keep your knees and shoulders covered. A *sarong* (the traditional cloth tied like a skirt) is mandatory. Many temples lend or rent them to you.

* **Respect for the surroundings:** Keep quiet and respect the space for what it is: a place of prayer and active spiritual connection.

* **The menstruation rule:** At the entrances there are signs restricting entry to women during menstruation. That shocked me quite a bit, but after reading the explanation, it is linked to the concept of *sebel* in Balinese Hindu cosmology, which is understood as: a state of spiritual vulnerability or temporary ritual impurity related to blood. It is presented as a norm of energetic balance and purity of the sacred space that is strictly observed.

## The Purification Ritual (Melukat)

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/ritual-espiritual.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Balinese purification ritual in sacred pools, Ubud</span></div>

Many of the temples are centered on purification through water, a sacred healing ritual performed in different pools with fountains according to the spiritual purpose of each water spout.

To do this ritual respectfully and correctly, you need a local guide who accompanies you, instructs you in the steps and helps you make the offerings. I know there's quite a bit of controversy and debate online about this and the truth is that for those of us who want to truly connect with this practice of faith, it's not at all pleasant to be haggling over money or watching out for possible scams. That's why here is the **information** that in my case was some of the **most relevant for the trip:**

* **General temple entrance:** The general entrance fee (for example, at the famous *Tirta Empul* temple) is around **75,000 IDR** per adult. It is paid at the official ticket window at the entrance. With your ticket they will lend you a basic sarong to walk around the complex, but remembering to keep your shoulders covered is your responsibility. **You do NOT need to buy a sarong at the stalls at the entrance.** If the vendors insist and you don't want to buy, simply thank them with a smile and keep walking toward the official ticket office.

* **The local guide:** A guide who explains the history and mystique of the place and accompanies you step by step in the pool is necessary to avoid disrespectful mistakes (also, we're not sure whether the ritual can be done without a guide). [You can contact certified guides in advance online](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/bali-l347/bali-visita-al-templo-gunung-kawi-con-ritual-de-purificacion-t1141894/?ranking_uuid=12e8c29b-2205-4eb2-ad7e-128b215afda8&q=ritual+de+purificacion&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08) or hire one at the temple entrance. Our advice for this second option is that you agree on their fee clearly before starting (we say this from experience). The price for their guide service is usually around **150,000 IDR** per person.
* **The Purification Ritual (extra costs):** Taking part in the sacred pools has additional requirements and costs that the general entrance does not cover:

  * **Ritual clothing:** It is mandatory to enter the water with a special purification sarong (you can't use the same dry sarong you walk around the temple in). Rental costs about **15,000 IDR**.

  * **Lockers:** To leave your belongings dry while you're in the water, rental costs **10,000 to 15,000 IDR** per person.

  * **Offerings (Canang Sari):** These are the small baskets of palm leaves with flowers and incense presented before entering the water. They cost about **10,000 to 20,000 IDR** (usually provided by the guide).

* **Estimated total of the experience:** approximately **260,000 to 275,000 IDR** (about 14.5-15.3 USD) to do the whole guided, respectful and complete purification ceremony, including the entrance fee, guide, rentals and offerings.

One option that seems reasonable to us after our experience, to avoid breaking the mystique of the moment, is to book in advance [a tour that has everything included.](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/bali-l347/bali-visita-al-templo-gunung-kawi-con-ritual-de-purificacion-t1141894/?ranking_uuid=12e8c29b-2205-4eb2-ad7e-128b215afda8&q=ritual+de+purificacion&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08) We find [this tour interesting, which includes the ritual, the entrance fees, the added costs and also lets you see the Jatiluwih terraces (the less touristy ones).](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/jatiluwih-l88105/bali-tour-por-las-terrazas-arroceras-de-jatiluwih-y-purificacion-del-agua-t1327978/?ranking_uuid=7a0f57c5-f655-4f07-ba90-7c8c509de41d&q=Terraza+de+arroz+de+Jatiluwih&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08)

## 6. Traditional dance: The power of Kecak and the elegance of Legong

The "Fire Dance" (*Kecak*) is striking for its frenzied rhythm, but the women's dances (*Legong*) have an elegance and a control of body movements. Originally, these dances were performed at royal courts in front of the nobility to tell founding myths. 

Both are performed at sacred temples: the [women's dance *Legong*](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/ubud-l32246/bali-ubud-palace-legong-dance-show-ticket-de-entrada-t440675/?ranking_uuid=1fc82157-37ce-418d-8f38-86f786139f4c&q=legong&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08) is performed daily at the palace and the Fire Dance *Kecak* changes depending on the day, but everything is in the surroundings of central Ubud. Both shows start at 7:30 pm, but it is recommended to arrive 30 minutes earlier to get a spot. Tickets can be bought online, at the entrance or from authorized street vendors who hand you the brochure and kindly answer all your questions. The ticket costs 100,000 IDR approx. 5.57 USD or 4.87 Euros and refreshments are sold at the entrance.

This time, [we chose the famous **Kecak Dance**](https://www.getyourguide.com/ubud-l32246/bali-entradas-para-el-espectaculo-de-danzas-kecak-y-del-fuego-de-ubud-t761650/?ranking_uuid=a25889a5-e5aa-4cf1-bb86-4ca8e92b23a2&q=ubud+Danza+Kecak%3A+la+danza+del+fuego&adults=1), an impressive cultural show with **more than 70 actors on stage**.

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/UBUD2.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Kecak dance: the fire dance night show at a temple in Ubud</span></div>

## [Kecak Dance: the fire dance](https://www.getyourguide.com/ubud-l32246/bali-entradas-para-el-espectaculo-de-danzas-kecak-y-del-fuego-de-ubud-t761650/?ranking_uuid=a25889a5-e5aa-4cf1-bb86-4ca8e92b23a2&q=ubud+Danza+Kecak%3A+la+danza+del+fuego&adults=1)

The curious thing about this piece is that it uses no musical instrument in the background. The sound, atmosphere and rhythm are created exclusively by a chorus of more than 70 men seated in concentric circles, who modulate their voices imitating different sounds and enter a kind of sonic trance throughout the piece (this is a personal reflection, because the way they expel air from the throat is very particular) and the constant rhythm sustains the show.

At the beginning, the dance narrates a fragment of the Hindu epic the *Ramayana*: the love story of Prince Rama and Princess Sita, her abduction at the hands of the evil multi-headed demon Ravana, and the subsequent mystical battle in which the brave army of monkeys led by the monkey god Hanuman intervenes. At the end (during the last 10 or 15 minutes of the show), the fire dance takes place, in which a dancer in a full state of mystical trance steps on and scatters burning coconut embers with his own bare feet.

[Tickets for the Fire Dance show](https://www.getyourguide.com/ubud-l32246/bali-entradas-para-el-espectaculo-de-danzas-kecak-y-del-fuego-de-ubud-t761650/?ranking_uuid=52efd32b-3783-48d5-a45f-37353c5a7dcf&q=volcan+bali&date_from=2026-07-08&date_to=2026-07-08)

## 7. Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary: The sacred home of the macaques

Located at the end of the busy Monkey Forest Road, this sacred forest is an ecological and spiritual reserve managed by the local community of Padangtegal. 

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/UBUD30.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Monkey with her babies in the Monkey Forest of Ubud, Bali</span></div>

This sanctuary is governed by the concept of *Tri Hita Karana* (harmony between humans, nature and God), safeguarding three active sacred temples from the 14th century which can be seen from outside but not entered. It's a flat route, easy to walk, an interesting local experience open to all ages. In addition, the path has quite a few trees so the heat isn't terrible, making it a more enjoyable experience.

**Entrance costs:** The ticket costs **130,000 IDR** for international adults and **100,000 IDR** for children (approximately 7.24 USD or 6.33 Euros for adults and 5.57 USD or 4.87 Euros for children). The park is open every day from 9:00 AM to 6:00 PM and has self-service kiosks that accept cards, or tickets can be bought online. There are also guides who can accompany you and explain the route of this or other points of interest.

**Strict safety rules:** Although long-tailed macaques are very sweet, they are still wild animals. For your safety and theirs, the sanctuary recommends:

* Do not enter with plastic bags or loose plastic bottles.
* Avoid getting too close, especially to those with their babies (although the truth is that we saw lots of them literally on top of people).
* Do not stare them in the eyes: in their body language, direct eye contact is a sign of aggression.
* Do not hide food in your backpack and be careful with personal items such as glasses or shiny objects, as they are very curious and trusting.

## 8. Ubud Palace (Puri Saren Agung)

<div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/palacio-real-ubud-bali.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Ubud Royal Palace during the day</span></div>

Located on the corner of Jalan Raya Ubud and Jalan Monkey Forest, opposite the traditional art market, Ubud Palace is the geographic and historical epicenter of the city. Built during the reign of Ida Tjokorda Putu Kandel (1800-1823), it still serves as the official residence of Ubud's royal family, so only its public outer courtyard and its intricate stone-carved portals decorated with gold leaf can be toured.

* **Daytime visit:** Entrance is completely **free** during the day (from 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM). It's a quick visit, about 10 or 15 minutes; I think it's a practical stop since it's in the center of town and lets you see up close the marvelous architecture of the classical Balinese court. There is also a restaurant inside. 
* **Night shows:** Every night, starting at 7:30 PM, the main courtyard is transformed into a torch-lit stage for the traditional Balinese dance of the *Legong* women.

*Editor's note: Dear reader, remember that prices and regulations in Bali fluctuate depending on the season and updates from the local administrations of each regency. It is recommended to always carry cash in Indonesian rupiah (IDR), since many temples and waterfalls do not accept credit cards.*

## **Where to stay in Ubud**

We tried several options, but my recommendation for those who want to enjoy the traditional calm of the rice fields, get to know the city and be close to the different areas to visit is the following: Staying in the center is an easy option, since it lets you walk to different restaurants and shows, although it should be said that it's a very touristy and heavily populated area.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to visit Ubud?

The best time is during the dry season, which runs from **April to October**. During these months there is less rain, the waterfall and rice terrace paths are in better condition and the weather is ideal for sightseeing.

### Is it safe to get around Ubud and its surroundings?

Yes, Ubud is an extremely safe destination for travelers, families and people traveling alone. You only need to take basic common-sense precautions, such as wearing a helmet if you rent a scooter, being careful with your valuables in the Monkey Forest and booking excursions with official guides. Take special care when walking along the street, since outside the center there are usually no sidewalks to walk on; in general, drivers are attentive, but it is advisable to walk during the day, carefully and in single file. 

### What is the best way to get around Ubud?

If you have experience, renting a **scooter** is the fastest and cheapest way (between 50,000 and 80,000 IDR a day). If you'd rather avoid the heavy traffic in the center or you don't ride a motorbike, you can hire a taxi or **hire a private driver for the day** to do waterfall and temple routes, or use apps like **Gojek/Grab** for short trips.

### How much does entrance to Ubud's Monkey Forest cost in 2026?

General admission for international adults costs **130,000 IDR** (approx. 7.24 USD / 6.33 EUR) and for children **100,000 IDR** (approx. 5.57 USD / 4.87 EUR). Tickets can be bought directly at the self-service machines or online.
