## Introduction and Useful Facts to Get Your Bearings

Nusa Penida is an island of approximately 200 km² located southeast of Bali's main island, about 20 kilometers across the Badung Strait. It forms part of a small archipelago made up of three neighboring islands: **Nusa Penida** (the largest), **Nusa Lembongan**, and **Nusa Ceningan**.

Just like in Bali, the vast majority of its local population (around 98%) practices **Balinese Hinduism**. This means ceremonies, *Canang Sari* (daily offerings), and community life operate the same way. If you're interested in better understanding the local worldview, traditions, and the meaning behind their rituals, we invite you to read our [complete guide on Bali's culture and soul](https://blogcito.com/diario/2026-07-22-el-alma-de-bali-rituales-familia-y-comunidad/). However, the island also hosts a small historical Muslim community.

Ever since we started planning our independent trip to Bali while chatting with friends who had already visited, Nusa Penida became a dream on our bucket list. After enjoying a few quiet days in Amed, we decided to embark on the adventure: **10 days on the island of paradises, beaches, and cliffs**. But the usual questions popped up: where to stay in Nusa Penida, Bali? What to see? How to escape the mass Instagram experience? Well, that was the challenge we're choosing to share today.

## Best Time to Visit: When to Go to Nusa Penida?

Nusa Penida has a tropical climate split into two distinct seasons, similar to main Bali:

* **Dry Season (May to October):** The **ideal time to travel**. Days are sunny, dirt roads are much more manageable, and the sea is clear for snorkeling and diving. **July and August** see the highest influx of international tourists (and are also the best months for spotting mantas in Manta Bay).
* **Rainy Season (November to April):** Rain showers are usually intense and short-lived. Secondary dirt and gravel roads become quite slippery and dangerous on a scooter. Underwater visibility may drop slightly due to swell.

## How to Get to Nusa Penida from Bali

The main sea access routes are operated via fast boats from various ports. Generally, morning sailings offer smooth waters; toward the afternoon, the sea tends to get a bit rougher due to wind.

### 1. Sanur Port (Pelabuhan Sanur)

The main and most modern pier for departing from Bali.

📍 **Google Maps Name:** *Pelabuhan Sanur* (Sanur Harbour Terminal).\
⏱️ **Boat Ride:** 30 to 45 minutes.\
🗺️ **Best choice if you're in:** Canggu, Seminyak, Kuta, Legian, Jimbaran, Nusa Dua, or Sanur.\
🚗 **Overland Transfer Time:**

* 30 min from Kuta/Seminyak
* 45–60 min from Canggu or Ubud  

💰 **Price:** 19 USD

### 2. Kusamba Port (Tribhuana Pier)

Our pick for being the fastest sea crossing and free from massive crowds. Coming from Amed, we bought a combo ticket (*Transport + Boat*) that included a van taking us straight to the pier. It was super practical and right on time.

📍 **Google Maps Name:** *Tribhuana Port Kusamba*.\
⏱️ **Boat Ride:** 15 to 20 minutes (the shortest crossing).\
🚗 **Overland Transfer:**

* 40–45 min from Ubud
* 20 min from Sidemen  

🗺️ **Best choice if you're in:** Ubud, Sidemen, Klungkung, or Candidasa.\
🎯 **Notes:** The port is quite basic and currently under construction (we had zero issues). Pros: less travel time, fewer people. Ideal if you get seasick easily.\
💰 **Price:** 150,000 IDR (10 USD) each way.

### 3. Padang Bai Port

The traditional harbor in East Bali (departure point for fast boats and the large *Roro* ferry capable of crossing heavy vehicles).

📍 **Google Maps Name:** *Padang Bai Port*.\
⏱️ **Boat Ride:** 30 min (fast boat).\
🚗 **Overland Transfer:**

* 1 hr 15 min from Ubud
* 1 hr 30 min from the south  

🗺️ **Best choice if you're in:** Padang Bai, Candidasa, or Amed.\
🎯 **Notice:** Nobody recommended it to us when selling tickets. Historically cheaper, but the public ferry charges a differential rate for foreigners.\
💰 **Price:** 150,000 IDR (10 USD).

### Crossings from Lombok and the Gili Islands

* **From Lombok:** Fast boat from Bangsal Port.
* **From the Gili Islands:** Fast boat from Gili Trawangan Port.

## Getting Around the Island: Scooter or Car with Driver?

Transportation on Nusa Penida is a crucial topic. **As mentioned, ride-hailing apps like Grab or Gojek do not work.** Roads have improved somewhat along main arteries, but secondary streets and those leading down to beaches remain in **very rough shape.**

* **Scooter Rental:** The most budget-friendly option, offering complete freedom. **Recommended only if you have prior experience riding scooters** on tricky terrain. Always wear a helmet, drive slowly, and inspect the brakes before setting off.
* **Car with Private Local Driver:** Hiring a car with a local driver for the day is the comfortable and safe alternative to explore the island stress-free. Most homestays offer this service, or [you can book it here](https://www.getyourguide.com/nusa-penida-l89276/nusa-penida-alquiler-de-coches-privados-con-conductor-t395604/?ranking_uuid=62756bc9-49b0-4fa3-b414-c422ce4be886&referral_redirect=1&q=Batoemadeg).

## Day Trip to Nusa Penida vs. Staying Overnight?

A day trip is a quick and easy way to catch a glimpse of the island's main highlights (detailed below). However, it comes with a few drawbacks:

* **Why we do NOT recommend a 1-day tour:** Speedboats disembark en masse between 9:30 AM and 10:30 AM. Between then and 3:30 PM, iconic spots become overcrowded, viewpoints fill up with people having photo sessions, and narrow roads get gridlocked with traffic, with reported waits of up to an hour.
* **Why we chose to stay overnight:** Being able to explore at our own pace, visiting major sights during off-peak hours (first thing in the morning or late afternoon), while enjoying the sunset and quiet island atmosphere once the sun goes down (mainly on the west coast). Plus, experiencing the island, its traditions, and village life on the east coast. Highly recommended for anyone interested in local culture and heritage.

### 🌊 Snorkeling in Nusa Penida: Swimming with Manta Rays

Nusa Penida has several snorkeling spots. The primary ones are **Crystal Bay, Manta Bay, Manta Point, Gamat Bay**, and **Wall Point**. Of all these, **only Crystal Bay can be accessed directly from the shore**. To reach the rest, you'll need to [book a boat excursion](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/s/?q=nusa+penida+snorkel+manta+bay&searchSource=3&src=search_bar&date_from=2026-08-12&date_to=2026-08-12).

The island's main highlight is the chance to swim alongside harmless giant manta rays measuring up to 4 meters across. At other spots along the route, you can also spot sea turtles, tropical fish, and vibrant coral reefs.

#### Where to Spot Mantas: Manta Point or Manta Bay?

The two experiences are completely different and depend heavily on what you're looking for (and how comfortable you are with open sea):

* **Manta Point:**

  * **Manta Sightings Chance:** 80% to 90% almost year-round.
  * **The Experience:** It functions as a "cleaning station," the exact open-ocean site mantas visit so small fish can pick parasites off them.
  * **To keep in mind:** It's an open-ocean outing; the water is often rough and colder. The boat ride is longer and bumpy, but the experience is unbelievable. [You can reserve your spot for this option here.](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/nusa-penida-l89276/isla-de-nusa-penida-snorkel-en-manta-point-y-tour-por-la-costa-oeste-t816548?date_from=2026-08-20)
* **Manta Bay:**

  * **Manta Sightings Chance:** Around 60% to 70% during the dry season (mainly July and August); percentages are lower in the rainy season. Here you can catch sight of a few mantas (ranging from small to giant).
  * **The Experience:** A deep bay framed by cliffs. Waves are present, but swells aren't as extreme as at Manta Point. We were lucky enough to see them here, and it was truly a once-in-a-lifetime moment. [You can book your 3-spot island snorkeling excursion here.](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/nusa-penida-l89276/nusa-penida-excursion-de-esnorquel-en-manta-bay-y-manta-point-t1393333/?ranking_uuid=f9ba3ef1-6f5e-4621-b41a-37677930ae94&q=nusa+penida+snorkel)

#### 💡 Essential Tips for Your Snorkeling Trip

* **What's included:** Virtually all tours include snorkeling gear (mask and snorkel) and life jackets (optional, but recommended if currents are strong). Guides also carry a GoPro to take photos that they share with you afterward.
* **Schedules:** Boats generally depart between 9:00 AM and 10:00 AM on shared boats with a sizable group (around 10 people in our case).
* **Locations:** Specific stops may adjust on the day depending on weather conditions (safety comes first), which you'll learn upon arrival.
* **Group Tip:** If you're traveling in a group of **4 or more**, hiring a private boat is a fantastic option. The cost works out to approx. $40 USD per person, giving you the freedom to discuss spots, set your own pace, and bypass large tour groups. [You can book it here](https://www.getyourguide.com/nusa-penida-l89276/nusa-penida-dia-completo-de-snorkel-privado-con-manta-raya-t700397?ranking_uuid=6bcc2e0d-8232-4586-9143-1377c7f6176c&q=tour+privado+snorkel+nusa+penida&date_from=2026-09-25&_pc=1,5).

### 📍 East & Southeast Coast

#### Diamond Beach and Atuh Beach

* **Our Rating:** 10/10
* **Cost:** Combined entry fee varies by entry point: from Atuh Beach 25,000 IDR; from Diamond Beach approx. 45,000 IDR.
* **Best time to go:** From 7:00 AM to 11:00 AM or after 3:30 PM.
* **The Experience:** Two of Indonesia's finest beaches sitting side by side. Diamond Beach is famed for bright white stone cliffs and brilliant turquoise water (unswimmable); Atuh Beach is our favorite on the island, offering calmer waters and peaceful *warungs* to relax on the sand.\
  *Note:* The trip involves plenty of steps: descending to Diamond Beach, entering Atuh Beach (not as many), and traversing from one zone to the other (steep with almost no handrails).

#### Treehouse (Rumah Pohon) & Thousand Islands Viewpoint

* **Our Rating:** 7.5/10
* **Cost:** Area admission: 10,000 IDR | Extra fee to climb up for the treehouse photo: 75,000 IDR.
* **Best time to go:** Early morning.
* **The Experience:** Dramatic sweeping views of the ocean and scattered islets. Very long queues form for the commercial photo on the cabin stairs. On-foot access requires climbing steep stairs.

#### Teletubbies Hills (Bukit Teletubbies)

* **Our Rating:** 7/10
* **Cost:** Entrance: 10,000 IDR.
* **Best time to go:** Early morning or sunset (best visited right after rainy season to catch them vibrant green).
* **The Experience:** Rolling green mounds in the island's interior reminiscent of the landscapes from the famous Teletubbies TV show. Perfect for a quick roadside pitstop.

#### Cave Temple (Pura Goa Giri Putri)

* **Our Rating:** 6/10
* **Cost:** Donation/Entry: 25,000 IDR | Sarong Rental: 10,000 IDR.
* **Best time to go:** Early (around 9:00 AM) or after 4:00 PM. It's not overly crowded with tourists, but heat and humidity inside the cave peak during midday.
* **The Experience:** One of the island's most sacred and active temples. Entry involves stairs and crouching through a narrow rock crevice, symbolizing rebirth from Mother Earth's womb. A site filled with deep spiritual energy (and home to an endangered species of blind endemic crab). It features several interior altars, including one dedicated to Buddhism. Wearing a *sarong* and covering your shoulders is mandatory. During our visit, we witnessed several ceremonies where groups of locals conducted prayer rituals before receiving blessings from the priest.

## Expectation vs. Reality: Is Nusa Penida Worth Visiting?

If you're chasing the picture-perfect postcard of Kelingking Beach or Diamond Beach, you'll certainly find it. But the reality behind the Instagram photo includes long lines for photo ops, waiting times (since it's rarely just one photo, but full photo shoots), and narrow roads gridlocked with cars and vans.

However, early in the morning and after 4:00 PM, the atmosphere shifts dramatically into pure peace and enjoyment.

Is Nusa Penida in Bali worth visiting? Our answer is a **resounding yes**, but carefully choosing when to explore if you want to enjoy it in peace. Doing a day trip is an option, provided you know it will be a rushed tour to snap photos (or catch a brief glimpse) of each spot. It's also worth noting that longer multi-day organized tours exist, such as this [3-day guided tour that also covers Nusa Lembongan](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/batoemadeg-l189824/tour-de-3-dias-por-nusa-penida-oeste-este-y-nusa-lembongan-t1424285?ranking_uuid=dae41b4b-8051-4e96-a198-b9ba36db8bb6&q=Rumah+Pohon+%22Casa+del+%C3%A1rbol%22,+Nusa+Penida&date_from=2026-09-23).

## Frequently Asked Questions About Traveling to Nusa Penida (FAQ)

### How many days do you need to see Nusa Penida?

We recommend setting aside a **minimum of 3 days / 2 nights** to cover the highlights (at a fast pace). However, the ideal timeframe to explore the island unhurried and enjoy both coastlines is **5 days / 4 nights**.

* *Digital Nomad Tip:* Staying 8 days by combining morning work sessions with afternoon exploration works out brilliantly.

### Can you swim at Nusa Penida's beaches?

At most cliffside beaches (like Kelingking or Broken Beach), currents are extremely strong and **swimming is neither allowed nor recommended**. For safe, calm swimming, your best options are Crystal Bay or Atuh Beach.

### Is riding a scooter in Nusa Penida dangerous?

Yes, inland roads are narrow, pitted with deep potholes, and feature sharp turns without guardrails. If you lack solid motorcycle experience, we strongly recommend hiring a car with a driver. It is doable for experienced drivers who exercise extreme caution.
