One December afternoon, a month after arriving in New Zealand, sipping mate and chatting with Lucy, a Kiwi friend, at the foot of Mount Maunganui, we decided to ask her, before our adventure south, which place in New Zealand she thought we couldn't miss. Her answer was emphatic: "Arthur’s Pass", a trek with alpine forest and dry tundra landscapes. From then on we decided we had to add it to our itinerary no matter what and, although it took a while, we discovered it was only two hours from Christchurch. A fantastic route with a spectacular DOC campsite, a space suitable for campervans with a 360° view of the mountain.

So one weekend we packed the mate and set off west of the city of Christchurch, toward the center of the South Island. Apparently, this pass was discovered by the Māori, who carried jade from the east coast to the west; later the English would use it during the gold rush. Today it is State Highway 73, a road laid along the junction of two still-active plates that tempts you to cross the South Island from east to west, resting at its campsites.

## History of Arthur's Pass: From the Māori to the gold rush

This pass was fundamental historically for the Māori on their pounamu (jade) trade routes, and later for European settlers during the gold rush era, becoming the vital route we know today as State Highway 73.

## Ecosystem and nature in Arthur's Pass

According to what we researched, the ecosystem is divided into different zones with distinct landscapes; this is more visible closer to summer, when the vegetation is more active:

* **Beech forest (East side - Canterbury):** On the slopes facing Christchurch, the climate is drier. The dominant ecosystem is the Mountain Beech forest. It's a more open forest, with less undergrowth and a tidier look.

  <div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/whatsapp-image-2026-06-27-at-20.45.55-9-.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption"></span></div>
* **Temperate rainforest (West side - West Coast):** Toward the west side, rainfall increases drastically. The ecosystem turns into a Rata and Kamahi temperate rainforest. It's extremely dense, green, full of mosses, ferns and epiphytes, very similar to a cold jungle.

  <div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-izquierda"><img src="/img/diario/whatsapp-image-2026-06-27-at-20.45.55-7-.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption"></span></div>
* **Alpine zone (Summits):** Above the treeline (approx. 1,200 meters), the ecosystem becomes alpine tundra or high-altitude grasslands. It's characterized by tussock (golden grasslands), megaherbs and the rocky outcrops where the Kea (the world's only alpine parrot) lives.

## Trail and trekking guide in Arthur's Pass

Even though it sits at 920 meters and you can feel the cold mountain air, the routes aren't particularly steep, and they come in different lengths and levels of difficulty. The condition of the paths, steps and signage make it a more accessible outing for everyone. There are several entrances because the circuit is not a loop; so some people were doing sections, leaving their vehicles at the different parking lots. 

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/whatsapp-image-2026-06-27-at-20.45.57.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption"></span></div>

**The total duration of the trails varies a lot: you can find short 30-minute walks or full-day treks (4 to 6 hours)**, but every trail has clear signage showing its length and duration. Some recommended trails are the **Devil's Punchbowl Track** (ideal for a short walk to the waterfall), the **Bealey Spur Track** (with incredible panoramic views) and **Avalanche Peak** (for those looking for a bigger challenge). In any case, the total duration of the trail is approximately 4 to 6 hours depending on the route you choose. I also recommend checking the weather in the exact area, since the climate is cool and mountain-like (with the unpredictable changes that come with that).

One last recommendation: a short stop along the way that is quite impressive.

## Kura Tāwhiti (Castle Hill): A natural spiritual center of New Zealand

It's an imposing set of giant limestone rocks that reach 30 meters in height. It looks like a stone labyrinth, and the story goes that millions of years ago it was under the sea. Over time, the seabed rose, creating these unique formations that even the Dalai Lama himself, when he visited them in 2002, dubbed the "spiritual center of the universe".

But beyond the anecdote, this is a sacred site for the Māori people, who call it Kura Tāwhiti ("the treasure of a distant land"), and it has "tōpuni status", a legal and spiritual mantle of protection that reminds us it is a place of deep history. Since 2022, the entrance has been guarded by three pou whenua (carved pillars) by master Riki Manuel, which honor the ancestors and invite us to walk with respect.

The path is short and friendly since it has no big slopes, but it's important to stay on the marked trail, for safety and also to protect the site. It's a site protected by New Zealand's Department of Conservation (DOC) and you can find out more on its official website. [Castle Hill / Kura Tāwhiti official page](https://thisisnewzealand.com/things-to-do-in-new-zealand/things-to-do-in-new-zealand-castle-hill)

We found both Arthur Pass National Park and Castle Hill to be a very beautiful area, with lots of trails to walk and a living history that speaks of how the land came to be as we know it today.
