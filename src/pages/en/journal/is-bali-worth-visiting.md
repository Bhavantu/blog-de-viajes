Bali had been on my bucket list for years, and in 2026, I was lucky enough to visit twice. It's a destination surrounded by sky-high expectations and mixed reviews. If you're currently planning a trip, here is our honest take—what blew us away and what fell short—so you can decide for yourself whether Bali is truly worth the journey.

## What We Loved About Bali

### 1. Balinese Culture and Warmth

While Bali is part of Indonesia, its cultural identity is entirely distinct. Most locals practice Balinese Hinduism, which weaves traditional Indian deities with local animism (ancestral veneration) and Mahayana Buddhism. Often called the "Island of the Gods" or the "Island of a Thousand Temples," spirituality here isn't a show for tourists—sacred rituals are woven into every single day, forming the living backbone of the local community. It is a way of life completely different from anything back home. Immersing ourselves in this heritage—staying at family-run guesthouses, witnessing sacred ceremonies, and visiting ancient temples—is the main reason we would return in a heartbeat. On top of that, the locals are remarkably kind, welcoming, and quick with a warm smile.

#### Must-Do Cultural Experiences:

* **Kecak Dance at Uluwatu Temple:** A mesmerizing performance of rhythmic vocal chanting and fire dancing, performed at sunset high atop a dramatic ocean cliff. The choreography dramatizes a pivotal chapter of the Ramayana depicting the eternal triumph of good over evil. * **Legong Dance (Dance of the Maidens) at Ubud Palace:** A classical Balinese dance performed by young dancers illustrating ancient folktales (such as the story of King Lasem), embodying divine grace, refinement, and feminine poise. For show schedules and details, take a look at our section on [traditional dance: the power of Kecak and the elegance of Legong](https://blogcito.com/diario/que-hacer-ubud-bali-que-ver-zonas-consejos-guia/#6-danza-tradicional-el-poder-del-kecak-y-la-elegancia-del-legong).

### 2. Nature and Landscapes

From lush jungle gorges and cascading waterfalls to rugged coastlines and vibrant marine ecosystems, Bali's natural setting is undeniably striking. While tourist hotspots have grown crowded, there are still plenty of untouched natural pockets that make the trip worthwhile.
<div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/virgin-beach.webp" alt="Travel journal photo"><span class="img-caption">Virgin Beach near Amed</span></div> 

#### The Best Beaches

The Uluwatu peninsula boasted the most stunning beaches we encountered, such as Thomas Beach, Padang Padang, and Melasti Beach (our personal favorite).

**Virgin Beach (Bias Tugel):** A gorgeous cove with clear turquoise water that was a treat to visit after exploring the Taman Ujung Water Palace and Tirta Gangga. We made the day trip from Amed, where we spent a significant portion of our stay.

#### Snorkeling and Scuba Diving

There are incredible snorkeling spots across the mainland and neighboring barrier islands:

* **Amed:** A quiet coastal fishing village offering three primary shore-entry snorkel spots where you can explore healthy coral gardens, sea turtles, and exotic marine life steps from the black sand:

  * **Jemeluk Bay:** Colorful coral reefs and a sunken underwater temple shrine.
  * **Lipah Beach:** Pristine visibility and frequent sea turtle encounters.
  * **Japanese Shipwreck (Banyuning):** A shallow shipwreck resting just a few meters off the beach, heavily encrusted with corals. You can read more in our comprehensive slow travel breakdown: [Things to Do in Amed: Realistic Slow Travel Guide & Budget](https://blogcito.com/diario/que-hacer-amed-que-ver-guia-presupuesto/).
* **Snorkeling with Manta Rays in Nusa Penida:** Floating alongside these gentle giants in open water was hands down one of the most awe-inspiring moments of our travels. The two prime viewing spots are Manta Bay and Manta Point, accessible either by staying on Nusa Penida or booking a boat charter from mainland Bali. Check out our complete transit and packing breakdown in our [complete guide on what to see in Nusa Penida](https://blogcito.com/diario/que-ver-en-nusa-penida-guia/).
* **Snorkeling with Sea Turtles in the Gili Islands:** This was an adventure strongly recommended by locals and fellow travelers that we made sure to prioritize on our second trip via fast boat.
* **Scuba Diving in Amed:** Amed is celebrated internationally for easy-access shore diving, featuring plenty of certified dive shops catering to both beginners and seasoned divers.

#### Waterfalls

The central highlands—particularly north of Ubud—are dotted with jungle waterfalls. We lost count of how many hidden falls the locals tipped us off to. Because the central region has no coastline, taking a dip in these crisp mountain pools is the perfect way to recharge. Standouts include Nungnung, Leke Leke, and Sekumpul.

### 3. The Food Scene

From humble family-run street stalls to trendy international restaurants, the culinary options here are virtually limitless. The hospitality is warm wherever you sit down. There are endless options for plant-based foodies, seafood lovers, and carnivores alike, catering to all travel budgets.
Bali also has a thriving cafe culture where grabbing a coffee is an experience in itself—spots like Akasa Cafe feature cliffside valley views that are tough to beat.

* **Everyday Local Classics:** Nasi Goreng (fried rice with vegetables and egg), Mie Goreng (stir-fried noodles), and Gado Gado (steamed veggies served with peanut dressing, fresh tofu, and tempeh).

#### Average Food Costs (2026):

* **Local Warung:** 25,000 to 50,000 IDR ($1.50 to $3.00 USD) per dish.
* **Western Restaurant or Specialty Coffee Shop:** 70,000 to 150,000 IDR ($4.50 to $10.00 USD) per dish.

### 4. A Destination That Adapts to Any Travel Style

Whether you're after wild nightlife or a peaceful family getaway, Bali readily molds itself to fit almost any itinerary.

* **Family Travel:** There are plenty of activities suited for travelers with kids, such as the [Sacred Monkey Forest Sanctuary](https://es.trip.com/travel-guide/attraction/bali/sacred-monkey-forest-sanctuary-80319?poitype=3&locale=es-es&curr=eur&allianceid=14893&sid=316394044&ppcid=ckid-41811202359_adid-811497939240_akid-kwd-303843193134_adgid-200668903081&utm_source=google&utm_medium=cpc&utm_campaign=23898460452&gad_source=1&gad_campaignid=23898460452&gbraid=0AAAAABn2eFKZGIMLbyMqRtxKU-Qcd5P40&gclid=CjwKCAjwu53SBhAhEiwAJzSLNoEMl1qpxXr8hj3ODpiHlh_Cd9QkI_o_dZzeHB_hXC6O4zXlF488_RoCyhMQAvD_BwE), gentle snorkeling with life vests, quiet beaches, and full-day pool fun at parks like [Waterbom Bali](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g297697-d386919-Reviews-Waterbom_Bali-Kuta_Kuta_District_Badung_Regency_Bali.html) in Kuta.

* **Nightlife and Beach Clubs:** If partying is what you're after, the island is packed with world-famous venues like Savaya in Uluwatu. Basing yourself in Canggu (the epicenter of digital nomads and late-night scenes) or Kuta guarantees endless bars, DJs, and live music.

## What We Didn't Love: Structural & Environmental Realities

We believe in sharing an honest perspective grounded in real experiences. We highlight these issues without pointing fingers, recognizing that infrastructure strains and environmental bottlenecks are complex, systemic challenges felt across the globe:

### Overcrowding and Unchecked Development

Being a world-famous bucket-list hub means navigating dense crowds and nonstop commercial development. On one hand, rapid tourism brings vital jobs and an abundance of food and stay options for visitors.
The downside is that unchecked urban sprawl directly impacts farmland and fragile ecosystems. In heavily developed spots like Kuta, paving over agricultural land without adequate stormwater drainage leads to routine street flooding during the wet season. Compounding this, waves of large foreign investment—often supported by lax or uneven enforcement and local corruption (an issue candidly brought up by several locals we spoke with)—make it tough for small, family-owned ventures to compete on equal footing.

### The Waste and Plastic Crisis

Waste management remains one of Indonesia's most pressing challenges, and the island is no exception. Ocean currents frequently wash massive amounts of marine plastic onto coastal beaches. While plastic pollution is a worldwide crisis, seeing it up close on tropical shores is disheartening.

* **Tips for a More Mindful Journey:** As travelers, small habits add up: travel with a reusable water bottle, turn down single-use plastics, and leave zero trash behind in natural areas.
  Just as importantly, choosing to eat at neighborhood warungs and hiring independent local guides keeps your travel spending directly within the communities that need it most.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How many days do you need in Bali?

For a first-time trip, we recommend at least 10 to 14 days. This gives you enough breathing room to combine Ubud's cultural core with snorkeling or beach downtime in Amed, plus a relaxed side trip to Nusa Penida or the Gili Islands without rushing through your days.

### When is the best time of year to visit Bali?

The dry season runs from May through October, offering sunny weather, lower humidity, and optimal conditions for mountain hikes and clear-water snorkeling. The rainy season (November to April) brings brief, torrential downpours; it also brings fewer crowds and noticeably lower prices on accommodation.

### Do you need to rent a scooter to get around?

Renting a scooter offers flexibility, but only do so if you already have solid experience riding two-wheelers—Bali's traffic can be hectic and unpredictable. In reality, hiring a private driver for full-day excursions or using ride-hailing apps like Grab and Gojek is safe, very affordable, and saves you the stress of navigating.
