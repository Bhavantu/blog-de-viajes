**From the road to "local life": a bit of stability during the Working Holiday Visa experience**

The good and the bad of traveling today is that we're flooded with information. A thousand people have already lived the experience. Personally, I use YouTube, blogs and artificial intelligence a lot when I'm planning a trip or getting to know a place that interests me. But in the end, honestly, the picture changes depending on each person's story. There are many cities, and each one has its pros and cons. I finally decided on Christchurch, a South Island city surrounded by beautiful landscapes, with beaches and hikes nearby, and a united, resilient community that has been rebuilding since the 2011 earthquake.

After a long vacation trip around the South Island, I was ready for a "local life" experience. While I traveled down the east coast, I analyzed cities and tested the job market. If you're at that stage of choosing a base, in this post I tell you about my experience and how I decided to stay in Christchurch.

## **Afraid of choosing a city? Why Christchurch was my best decision in New Zealand**

Before ending up in Christchurch, I visited and looked at other options you've probably heard of. Each one has its own character, and although I visited both coasts, here is a short comparison of the South Island's big cities for living in New Zealand in 2026, east coast.

## 1. Living in Queenstown: advantages, costs and the accommodation problem

Queenstown is a dreamlike city with a vibrant, touristy atmosphere. It's surrounded by the majestic Southern Alps and sits on the shores of the crystal-clear Lake Wakatipu. The city shines in winter as much as in summer, with street performers, food stalls and incredible views from the main (pedestrian) square facing the lake, where the performers, food stalls and music accompany your walk along the shore. I also noticed people doing slackline and exercising in the entrance plaza, which, from my point of view, was a plus for living here: a relaxed, social atmosphere.

The center is packed with restaurants and hotels where you can eat well, with a lot of variety and international food. Dishes start at $20 NZD. I tried authentic Pad Thai and an Argentine ice cream shop in front of the lake; both moments were quite memorable. On the other hand, for anyone who wants to work, there are plenty of jobs in the service sector.

Something we heard from other travelers: in June there is a clear trend of people moving toward the ski fields to work the winter season, so you need to apply for jobs and book accommodation in advance.

### Pros and cons we noticed and that other people told us about:

* You have to book a place ahead of time: housing can be a real challenge for people who live in Queenstown and don't have a job that includes *accommodation*. This is mainly because there aren't many affordable, functional Holiday Parks nearby. Other travelers also told us that you need to secure a room well in advance, because demand is high in the winter season and costs tend to be a bit higher.
* Cons: It's more expensive and quite small, with fairly steep slopes on its central streets.
* Pro: It's very beautiful and has an interesting level of social activity.
* Pro 2: There is a direct transfer to Milford Sound, which for me is one of the must-sees in New Zealand.
* It's considered the world capital of adventure and extreme sports.

Travel tip: if you want to work at a ski field, keep in mind that although they open in June, **searches and contracts usually close in March or April**.

### 2. Dunedin: Victorian architecture and authentic local life

This city, nicknamed "Daniden", feels quite local and authentic. Honestly, it gave us a good feeling even before we visited: in Roxburgh we met a couple from Dunedin who were camping at a beautiful lake. They were cheerful, friendly and relaxed, and they spoke so well of their city that it made us curious to see it.

When we visited, we discovered its Victorian architecture in dark brick, its specialty coffee shops, street art and independent galleries. I get the sense that it's a city where people really live their day-to-day lives. Since it isn't as focused on mass tourism, the restaurants are more take-away style, geared to the local routine, which makes it a cheaper everyday option than Queenstown; definitely an interesting possibility.

### 3. Working and living in small mountain towns: an outdoor alternative:

I don't want to leave out the small towns, villages and countryside areas, which offer other ways of living far from the cities. Maybe it's the kind of experience that just happens along the way, but there are many beautiful towns near the cities above with other kinds of job offers and accommodation options, whether outdoor jobs or in other fields. The possibilities are wide open for anyone who dares to dream of living in a mountain town.

## 4. My final decision: Living in Christchurch on a Working Holiday in 2026

Christchurch is a city that is rebuilding, with history, community and spectacular views. You can see the Port Hills from the city, and their hiking trails are only twenty minutes away, as are two different beaches about half an hour away. The imposing Southern Alps and the incredible **Arthur's Pass (which you can read more about in our article)**, or some ski fields, are just two hours away by car. From my point of view, this city is the perfect balance between urban social life and constant escapes into nature.

The city is rebuilding after the major 2011 earthquake, so its infrastructure is modern and the layout of the city is convenient. It's a growing city with universities, new construction, a wide food scene and a growing population. Emi, our local friend, told us that many people he knows are moving to this city looking for new opportunities. From my perspective, although you have to focus on the job search and keep at it, I think it's big enough to offer opportunities for everyone.

**Advantages:** I'd highlight its modern infrastructure, the strong development of the service sector and the wide variety of accommodation available.

**Disadvantages:** it's an area of high seismic activity, although it's very well organized in terms of safety protocols and you can check the relevant apps, and it's definitely something to keep in mind. On the other hand, the density of travelers and residents can sometimes work against you when looking for a job (honestly, I was lucky and found work in 2 weeks, but I know people who took 2 months even with perfect English).
