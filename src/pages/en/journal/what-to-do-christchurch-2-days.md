Christchurch is a city with local vibe, specialty coffee shops, cultural nooks, nature, and modern architecture. Without a doubt, it's one of my favorite cities in New Zealand. If you read my article on why choose Christchurch for your Working Holiday, you know that this city has a unique energy of resilience and community based on its recent history. It's not just a grey transit city.

Here's How to Spend Your Time in Christchurch: A 2-Day Itinerary After Living There for 6 Months with a Working Holiday Visa

Structured as a two-day armed tour so you don't miss anything, with all the information so you can put together your own itinerary if you want.

Whether you're passing through or residing, these plans are adapted for all those who want to enjoy the city and its surroundings. Following is the Christchurch itinerary.

## Technical Travel Itinerary

**Key Information:**

* **Suggested Duration:** 2 full days (ideal for setting up base and exploring the surroundings or scaling back to get to know the city).
* **Average Budget:** Mid-Low (many free plans for nature and culture).
* **Getting Around:** On foot in the center, electric scooters (*Lime* or *Beam*), car, or urban buses.
* **Recommended Accommodation:** City center (practical for daily transportation) or Sumner beach for an incredible experience. 
* **Recommended Time:** All year round (spring/summer for outdoor strolls, adaptable for plans in autumn and winter).

## 2. Morning Afternoon: Stroll around the Arts Centre and city memorial

The **Arts Centre** of Christchurch is a complex of Gothic-style buildings. It was quite battered in the major earthquake of 2011, but today it's restored and full of life with art galleries, shops, and cafes.

**🔥 Travel Tip:** Sometimes the area comes alive with color at street events, such as the *Flare Street Art Festival* or the *World Buskers Festival*, which I had the pleasure of attending. You can check what dates coincide with your trip on the official ChristchurchNZ tourism and events page.

Walking a few minutes from the Arts Centre you can discover:

* **ChristChurch Cathedral:** The iconic Anglican church in Cathedral Square that continues in its historic process of reconstruction after the earthquake.
* **Canterbury Earthquake National Memorial:** The iconic white marble wall located by the Avon River, which commemorates the victims of the 2011 earthquake. I visited this place on the anniversary of the tragedy; seeing families with flowers in silence really made me connect with being thankful for life, which is ultimately unpredictable,
* **Christchurch Art Gallery Te Puna o Waiwhetū:** The city's contemporary art museum. I highly recommend it, famous for its wavy glass facade and avant-garde exhibits. The visit is quick, free of charge, and offers interesting collections from Pacific Island artists.

* **Canterbury Museum Pop-Up (Gloucester St):**Actualmente (2026) este museo mudó sus piezas más a un formato pop-up gratuito en el edificio de 66 Gloucester St. Es chiquito pero interesante, lleno de muestras de dinosaurios y propuestas interactivas para todas las edades. Como por ejemplo, un esqueleto de T-Rex, cajones para visualizar insectos con una lupa digital, un arenero para desenterrar los "fosiles". Muchos niños y no tan niños disfrutamos de la propuesta.

### Where to Eat in Christchurch

Christchurch is a city that loves its food, and you'll find no shortage of great places to eat, from casual cafes to fine dining restaurants. Here are some of our top recommendations:

* **The Good Sort**: This popular cafe serves up delicious breakfast and brunch options, as well as excellent coffee.
* **Shirley's**: For a taste of old-school New Zealand, head to Shirley's for their famous fish and chips or burgers.
* **Cashel Blue**: If you're looking for something a bit more upscale, Cashel Blue offers stunning views of the city along with its menu of modern NZ cuisine.

Of course, there are many more options to explore in Christchurch, but these three should give you a great starting point. Bon appétit!

The city's gastronomic pole is located near the downtown area, a clear example being the **Riverside Market**, a semi-covered market built overlooking the river. It's full of culinary options that range from international food from all corners of the world to local bakeries, craft beer bars, and small shops at the entrance. Its hours are quite extensive and some recommended options are:

* **Midnight Shanghai:** One of my favorites. It's a restaurant and *cocktail bar* serving *dim sum* and modern Asian cuisine with karaoke rooms on the second floor.

* **Kaiser Brew Garden:** Un jardín cervecero ubicado en el piso superior con buenas pizzas a la piedra y cervezas tiradas bien frías.
* **El Fogón:** Para quienes aman el asado, este puesto ofrece cortes de carne al estilo argentino tradicional y otros platos de la región.

### Other options outside the center

* **C1 Espresso (Tuam St & High St):** Functions in the old post office building of the city. The fun thing about this industrial-style café is that you toast your own food while waiting for burgers or fries, which are sent from the kitchen directly to your table through compressed air pneumatic tubes at 140 km/h that drop down from the ceiling.
* **The Cookie Trader (124 Cashel St):** A small specialty shop focused on giant cookies filled with Oreo cheesecake, salted caramel with pretzel or marshmallows. The charm is that you can customize to your taste, very "fancy".
* **Rollickin Gelato (New Regent St & Cashel St):** Makes artisanal ice cream using organic milk from local Canterbury farms. The shop has a unique style with old vinyl records recovered from the street and a free-flowing chocolate fountain to dip cones in.

## 4. Afternoon: The Botanical Garden and the River Avon

Christchurch has the nickname "the garden city" and it's due to its green squares and the vast **Botanical Garden**, which is attached to the river. It's the perfect place to throw yourself onto the grass to rest, read a book, go for a run on Sundays or play golf. Personally, it was my walking corner after work, it's literally so big that every day I would walk through a different area. It has a greenhouse, a small museum that tells about the island's flora and some history about gardens, and it's also the venue for free music festivals in summer.

* **Avon River Experience:** The [Punting on the Avon](https://www.getyourguide.com/christchurch-l1013/christchurch-punting-on-the-avon-t42475/?partner_id=TU_ID_AFILIADO) (traditional wooden boat ride with a guide dressed in period attire) is a classic that offers a different perspective of the city by navigating the River that runs through most of it.

If you continue walking along the River Avon, crossing the iconic *Bridge of Remembrance* takes you directly to **The Strip** (in Oxford Terrace). This pedestrian waterfront concentrates the city's main gastronomic offer; here locals and tourists meet to enjoy terraces and take a drink at top bars like *The Craft Embassy*, *Amazonita*, or *The Terrace Tavern*.

Christchurch has a quite lively nightlife on weekends. To help you organize the outing, here's the perfect night progression from lowest to highest intensity, you can choose according to your interests:

### Rooftops at Sunset (Epic Views)

Before the sun sets, the best option is to head up and see the mountain range and sky colors from the high altitudes:

* **Lounge 505 (Cathedral Square):** Located on the rooftop of Christchurch City Hotel, it's an excellent spot to grab a drink with 270-degree views of the cathedral and historic heart. They have a professional sommelier.
* **Pink Lady Rooftop Bar:** On the eighth floor of the Manchester Street building. Offers the best city-wide views in 360 degrees. Author cocktails are sophisticated and mid-range priced.

If the night asks for a more tranquil, intimate, and aesthetic atmosphere, your destination is the street of pastel-colored cottages:

* **Gin Gin:** A very popular option with a carefully tended botanical decor and an infinite variety of gin tonics featuring local New Zealand herbs.

### Live Music and Open Floor

To dance, these are my favorite options:

* **Fat Eddie's:** A classic that never fails. Located on the first floor above the Strip (Oxford Terrace), the bar offers live music almost every night (lots of jazz, soul, funk and cover bands with dance and shaking weekends). It's open until 3:00 am and usually fills up with pretty young people.
* **Muy Muy (Welles Street):** A Mexican kitchen bar in a trendy industrial area. It's ideal for those who love salsa, bachata, and kizomba; many dance schools come on open days and the real Latin party gets going.

### Local Secret of the Night: The Austin Club

Austin's oldest nightclub has been a staple in the city's vibrant music scene for over 125 years. Located on the first floor of the historic Driskill Hotel, **The Austin Club** is more than just a bar – it's an institution that embodies the spirit of Texas hospitality and live music.

Finally, I introduce you to this secret that a local couple showed us one night with friends. Hidden in an alleyway without exit from the city center (with zero charm on the outside) is **The Austin Club**, an underground *speakeasy* ambiented in a 1950s basement. The atmosphere is dark, with jazz playing in the background and a sophisticated cocktail menu (and very good).

> **Fact:** To get in, you need to search for the weekly password they post on their Instagram account and tell it to the doorman at the entrance before going down the stairs. Yes, very movie-like.

Located a bit further towards the suburb of Woolston, **The Tannery** is a boutique shopping center recycled from an old Victorian-era tannery industrial site. It's the perfect place to browse independent bookstores, vintage clothing stores, and breakfast at a local café. The whole aesthetic is very carefully curated, combining cast iron, wood, and rounded glass panes.

### 7. Afternoon: A vintage movie or a sunset stroll

Within The Tannery lies **Deluxe Cinemas**, a two-screen art house cinema designed like the cinemas of the 1920s. It has red velvet armchairs, polished wooden tables and a bar service with local wines while you watch the movie. It's a lifesaver for a rainy winter afternoon.

If the day cooperates, another beautiful option is to hike up to the **Port Hills** and take a walk in the evening to see the sun set over the Canterbury plains or a great option for my taste is:

### 8. The Christchurch Cable Car: Gondola and Crater Rim Walk

To see the city and the sea at the same time, the Christchurch Gondola is a cable car that lifts you above the slopes of *Mount Cavendish* in the Port Hills. As it rises, the panoramic view is 360 degrees that takes in from the Canterbury Plains and Southern Alps to Lyttelton Harbour and the Pacific Ocean.

* **For trekking enthusiasts:** At the foot of the gondola (Heathcote Valley) begins the historic *Bridle Path Walk* (a steep and demanding 1.5-hour trek that recreates the path taken by early settlers from Lyttelton to Christchurch). At the top, it connects with the *Crater Rim Walkway*, featuring natural lookout points over the rocky rim of the extinct volcano crater.*

## 🗺️ Other unmissable escape options: in surrounding areas

## Akaroa and Hector's Dolphin Spotting

Only an hour and a half's drive from the city, crossing the green valleys of the Banks Peninsula, you'll reach Akaroa, a charming and historic coastal town with French heritage (you'll see French flags and street names like *Rue Jolie*).

This town is nestled in a harbor that originally was the crater of an enormous extinct volcano. You can book a [Héctor's Dolphin Watching](https://www.getyourguide.com/akaroa-l1983/akaroa-2-hour-harbor-nature-cruise-t66952/?partner_id=TU_ID_AFILIADO) tour to navigate the volcanic fjord and see up close the world's smallest and rarest marine dolphins (they have a rounded dorsal fin like Mickey Mouse's ear). Seeing them jump alongside the boat is an unforgettable experience.

## The TransAlpine Train and Arthur's Pass Mountain Crossing

If you like high mountain scenery, this day trip is a must. You can take the famous [TranzAlpine](https://www.getyourguide.com/christchurch-l1013/christchurch-tranzalpine-train-journey-t394220/?partner_id=TU_ID_AFILIADO), considered one of the most scenic rail journeys in the world. The train departs early from Christchurch, crosses Canterbury's plains and enters the Waimakariri River gorges until it reaches the alpine heartland in the town of **Arthur's Pass**.

If you're basing yourself in the town or driving there, don't miss the walk to *Devil's Punchbowl*, a spectacular 131-meter waterfall surrounded by native forest. Keep your eyes open: it's very likely that you'll cross paths with the *Kea*, the world's only mountain parrot, known for being extremely curious (and for trying to bite car tires!).

## The International Antarctic Centre

Located next to the international airport, the **International Antarctic Centre** is a fascinating interactive theme and scientific park. Christchurch has historically been one of the five official entry points to Antarctica (missions from the United States and New Zealand depart from here). You can experience a real polar storm inside an ice dome with temperatures below zero, take a walk in an amphibious all-terrain vehicle *Hagglund* original, and get up close and personal with little blue penguins in their rescue center.

## Practical details for staying in Christchurch

### Where to stay (Recommended accommodations)

If you're arriving with a Working Holiday visa or planning your departure from Southern Island abroad, staying in the city center will save you a tremendous amount of money on daily transportation:

* **For backpackers / Working Holiday:** I recommend hostels on the first line in the center like **Urbanz** (with excellent common areas) or the original **Jailhouse Accommodation** (a historic prison remodeled and super cozy).
* **LyLo capsules:** If you want something modern, functional, and hyper-comfortable, [LyLo Christchurch](https://www.booking.com/hotel/nz/jucy-snooze-christchurch.html?aid=TU_ID_AFILIADO) is one of the most popular options in the country for its private capsule rooms that allow you to sleep with total privacy at a super budget-friendly cost.
* **For independent travelers:** If you prefer more privacy without losing the strategic location, you can search for [central hotels in Christchurch through Booking](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.en.html?city=-1508201&aid=TU_ID_AFILIADO) to be just steps away from trams and move entirely on foot.

### Transportation and Connectivity

**From the airport to the city center:** The express bus line (line 3 or *Purple Line*) takes you straight to the *Bus Interchange downtown* for just a few dollars. You can also opt for Uber or authorized taxis at the terminal exit.
**To get around the city:** Walking is the most lovely option, but if you're in a hurry, using electric skateboards for rent (like *Lime* or *Beam*) is the fastest way to cross from the Botanical Garden to the Arts Centre in just minutes.

> **💪 A prepared traveler is worth twice as much!** Visa requirements and tourist pass prices in New Zealand are changing; I recommend always checking the official information before traveling.
