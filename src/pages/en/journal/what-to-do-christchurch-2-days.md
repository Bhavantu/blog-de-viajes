Christchurch is a city with a local vibe, specialty coffee shops, cultural corners, nature and modern architecture. Without a doubt, it's one of my favorite cities in New Zealand. If you read [my article on why to choose Christchurch to kick off your Working Holiday](/en/journal/christchurch-working-holiday-jobs-costs/), you already know this city has a unique energy of resilience and community rooted in its recent history. It's not just a gray city you pass through. 

In this article I'm sharing everything worth doing in Christchurch after living there for 6 months on my Working Holiday Visa. It's structured as a two-day route so you don't miss anything, with all the information you need to build your own itinerary if you'd rather. 

Whether you're just passing through or living here, these plans work for anyone who wants to enjoy the city and its surroundings. Here are the best plans in Christchurch.

## 📊 Trip fact sheet

Key information:

* **Suggested duration:** 2 full days (ideal as a base for exploring the surroundings, or as a stopover to get to know the city).
* **Average budget:** Medium-low (lots of free nature and culture plans).
* **Getting around:** On foot in the center, electric scooters (*Lime* or *Beam*), car or city buses.
* **Recommended accommodation:** City center (practical for daily transport) or Sumner beach for an incredible experience. 
* **Best time to go:** All year round (spring/summer for outdoor walks, adaptable with plans for autumn and winter).

## Day 1

## 1. Historic center, art and local markets

## Morning: The city layout and the historic area

The city center is bounded by four main avenues (Bealey, Fitzgerald, Moorhouse and Deans Avenue). This area is lovely for walking and exploring. Like the famous **New Regent Street**, a pedestrian street of pastel-toned Spanish Mission-style houses (cover photo) along which runs the iconic [Christchurch Historic Tram. ](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/christchurch-l945/tour-de-la-ciudad-de-christchurch-en-tranvia-antiguo-t58043/?ranking_uuid=be48381e-37b2-4133-8c70-ec65169a19af&q=Tranv%C3%ADa+Hist%C3%B3rico+de+Christchurch.&adults=1)

If you keep walking toward the civic center you'll come across some iconic spots such as:

* **Cathedral Square:** In the middle of the city, it is the most important historic cathedral, currently under reconstruction after the 2011 earthquake. 
* **Bridge of Remembrance:** A monumental stone arch that pays tribute to the soldiers fallen in the wars (World War I, World War II and later armed conflicts) and serves as a pedestrian entrance to the riverside food area (point 5).
* **Victoria Square:** A park with fountains and historic statues, a beautiful walk surrounded by trees, greenery and the Avon River.

## 2. Mid-morning: A stroll through the Arts Centre and the city memorial

The **Arts Centre** in Christchurch is a complex of Gothic Revival buildings. It was hit pretty hard in the big 2011 earthquake, but today it's restored and full of life, with art galleries, shops and cafés.

**💡 Travel tip:** Sometimes the area fills with color thanks to street events, such as the *Flare Street Art Festival*, a street art festival, or the *World Buskers Festival*, a pay-what-you-want street performers festival that I had the pleasure of seeing. You can check which dates coincide with your trip on the official ChristchurchNZ tourism and events page.

A few minutes' walk from the Arts Centre you can discover:

* **Christchurch Cathedral:** The emblematic Anglican church in Cathedral Square, still going through its historic reconstruction after the quake.
* **Canterbury Earthquake National Memorial:** The emblematic white marble wall next to the Avon River, which commemorates the victims of the 2011 earthquake. I visited on the anniversary of the tragedy; seeing families quietly holding flowers made me truly connect with being grateful for life, which after all is unpredictable.
* **Christchurch Art Gallery Te Puna o Waiwhetū:** The city's contemporary art museum. I recommend it 100%; it's famous for its undulating glass facade and its cutting-edge exhibitions. The visit is quick, free, and offers interesting collections by artists from the Pacific Islands.

  <div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/museo-de-arte-chrischurch.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Christchurch Art Gallery Te Puna o Waiwhetū</span></div>
* **Canterbury Museum Pop-Up (Gloucester St):** Currently (2026) the museum has moved much of its collection into a free pop-up format in the building at 66 Gloucester St. It's small but interesting, full of dinosaur displays and interactive activities for all ages. For example, a T-Rex skeleton, drawers for looking at insects through a digital magnifying glass, and a sandpit for digging up "fossils". Plenty of kids, and not-so-kids, enjoyed it.

## 3. Where to eat in Christchurch

The city's food hub is near the center, and a clear example is **Riverside Market**, a semi-covered market built facing the river. It's full of food options ranging from international cuisine from every corner of the world to local bakeries, craft beer bars and small shops at the entrance. Its opening hours are quite long and some recommended options are: 

* **Midnight Shanghai:** One of my favorites. It's a *dim sum* and modern Asian cuisine restaurant and *cocktail bar*, with karaoke rooms on the second floor.

  <div class="post-img-box img-pequeño img-izquierda"><img src="/img/diario/mishnight-shanghai.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Going out to eat, private karaoke in central Christchurch, New Zealand</span></div>
* **Kaiser Brew Garden:** A rooftop beer garden on the upper floor with good stone-baked pizzas and ice-cold draft beers.
* **El Fogón:** For those who love asado, this stall offers traditional Argentine-style cuts of meat and other dishes from the region.

### Other options outside the center

* **C1 Espresso (Tuam St & High St):** Housed in the city's old post office building. The fun thing about this industrial-feel café is that you toast your own food on the side while you wait for the burgers or fries, which are sent from the kitchen straight to your table through compressed-air pneumatic tubes at 140 km/h that come down from the ceiling.
* **The Cookie Trader (124 Cashel St):** A small shop specializing in giant cookies filled with Oreo cheesecake, salted caramel with pretzel, or marshmallows. The fun part is that you can customize them to your taste, all very "fancy".
* **Rollickin Gelato (New Regent St & Cashel St):** They make artisanal ice cream using organic milk from farms in the Canterbury area. The shop has a distinctive style, with old armchairs salvaged from the street and a free-flowing tap of melted chocolate to dip your cone in. 

## 4. Afternoon: The Botanic Gardens and the Avon River

Christchurch is nicknamed "the Garden City", thanks to its green squares and the huge **Botanic Gardens**, right next to the river. It's the perfect place to lie on the grass and rest, read a book, go for a run on Sundays or play golf. Personally, it was my after-work walking spot; it's literally so big that every day I walked a different area. It has a greenhouse, a small museum about the island's flora and some history of the gardens, and it also hosts free music festivals in summer.

* **Avon River experience:** The [Punting on the Avon](https://www.getyourguide.com/christchurch-l1013/christchurch-punting-on-the-avon-t42475/) (a ride in a traditional wooden punt with a guide dressed in period costume) is a classic that gives you a different perspective of the city as you glide along the river that runs through much of it. 

If you keep walking along the Avon River and cross the iconic *Bridge of Remembrance*, you'll arrive straight at **The Strip** (on Oxford Terrace). This pedestrian riverside promenade has the city's biggest food and drink offering; this is where locals and tourists meet to enjoy the terraces and have a drink at top bars like *The Craft Embassy*, *Amazonita* or *The Terrace Tavern*.

## 5. A night of drinks, live music and secret basements

Christchurch has a pretty active nightlife on weekends. To help you plan your night out, here's the perfect night in order from least to most intense, so you can choose according to your interests:

### Sunset drinks (rooftops with epic views)

Before the sun goes down, the best option is to head up high and watch the mountain range and the colors of the sky:

* **Lounge 505 (Cathedral Square):** On the terrace of the Christchurch City Hotel, it's an excellent spot for a drink with 270-degree views of the cathedral and the historic heart of the city. They have a professional sommelier.
* **Pink Lady Rooftop Bar:** On the eighth floor of the building on Manchester Street. It offers the best 360-degree views of the whole city. The signature cocktails are sophisticated and mid-to-high budget.

### Beer and socializing:

If you're after something relaxed to hang out with friends, talk about life or plan the next trails: 

* **The Church:** The place where we met up with most of the friends I made during my Working Holiday. In a converted old church, this pub has live music, quiz nights, a heated patio for winter and good vegetarian food options.

  <div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-izquierda"><img src="/img/diario/the-church-.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">The Church, the temple of Working Holiday socializing</span></div>
* **Paddy McNaughton’s:** Practically next door to *The Church* is this classic Irish pub with the same owner. They serve the best Irish gin I've tried and pints of Guinness (among the cheapest in the center). I also found out that on weekends upstairs they hold "Irish parties" in a tiny space: a musician sings traditional songs, everyone sings and dances, and honestly it's an atmosphere of pure joy, and super inclusive, everyone's invited to share and dance with the crowd.

### Intimate and sophisticated corners (New Regent Street)

If the night calls for a calmer, more intimate and stylish atmosphere, head to the street of pastel-colored little houses:

* **The Last Word:** A lounge specializing in whiskies from around the world and classic cocktails. A very calm and quiet place.
* **Gin Gin:** A very popular option for its carefully curated botanical decor and an endless variety of gin and tonics with local New Zealand herbs.

### Live music and open dance floor

For dancing, these are the options I liked the most:

* **Fat Eddie's:** A classic that never fails. On a first floor above the Strip (Oxford Terrace), the bar has live music almost every night (lots of jazz, soul, funk and cover bands, with dancing and a buzz on weekends). It's open until 3:00 am and tends to fill up with a fairly young crowd.
* **Muy Muy (Welles Street):** A Mexican-food bar in a trendy industrial area. It's ideal for those who love salsa, bachata and kizomba; many dance schools show up on open dance floor nights and the real Latin party gets going.

### The night's local secret: The Austin Club

Finally, let me introduce you to this secret that a local couple showed us one night out for drinks with friends. Hidden in a dead-end alley in the center (looking like nothing from the outside) is **The Austin Club**, an underground *speakeasy* (secret bar) set in a 1950s-style basement. The look is dark, with jazz playing in the background and a menu of sophisticated (and very good) drinks.

> **Note:** To get in, you need to look up the weekly password they post on their Instagram account and tell it to the doorman at the entrance before heading down the stairs. Yes, straight out of a movie.

## Day 2

## 6. Morning: Industrial style at The Tannery

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/the-tannery.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">The Tannery</span></div>

Located a bit further out toward the suburb of Woolston, **The Tannery** is a boutique shopping center converted from an old Victorian industrial tannery. It's the perfect place to browse independent bookshops and vintage clothing stores, and to have breakfast at a local café. The whole aesthetic is very carefully done, combining cast iron, wood and rounded stained glass.

### 7. Afternoon: A period cinema or a sunset walk

Inside The Tannery you'll find **Deluxe Cinemas**, a two-screen arthouse cinema designed like the cinemas of the 1920s. It has red velvet armchairs, polished wooden tables and a bar serving local wines while you watch the movie. It's a lifesaver for a rainy winter afternoon.

If the weather cooperates, another lovely option is to head up the **Port Hills** for a sunset walk and watch the sun go down over the Canterbury plains, or a great option, to my taste, is:

### 8. The Christchurch cable car: the Gondola and the crater trail

To see the city and the sea at the same time, the Christchurch Gondola is a cable car that lifts you above the slopes of *Mount Cavendish* in the Port Hills. As it climbs, the 360-degree panoramic view takes in everything from the Canterbury plains and the Southern Alps to Lyttelton Harbour and the Pacific Ocean.

* **For trekking lovers:** At the foot of the gondola (Heathcote Valley) starts the historic *Bridle Path Walk* (a steep and demanding 1.5-hour trek that retraces the route the first settlers took from Lyttelton to Christchurch). At the top it connects with the *Crater Rim Walkway*, with natural lookouts over the rocky edges of the extinct volcano's crater.

## 🗺️ Other unmissable getaways: in the surrounding areas

## Akaroa and Hector's dolphin watching

Just an hour and a half by car from the city, crossing the green valleys of the Banks Peninsula, you reach Akaroa, a charming and historic coastal town with French heritage (you'll see French flags and street names like *Rue Jolie*).

This town sits in a harbor that was originally the crater of an enormous extinct volcano. You can book a [Hector's dolphin watching](https://www.getyourguide.com/akaroa-l1983/akaroa-2-hour-harbor-nature-cruise-t66952/) cruise to sail through the volcanic fjord and see up close the smallest and rarest marine dolphins in the world (they have a rounded dorsal fin like Mickey Mouse's ear). Watching them jump next to the boat is an unforgettable experience.

## The TranzAlpine train and Arthur's Pass mountain pass

If you like high mountains, this day trip is a must. You can take the famous [TranzAlpine train](https://www.getyourguide.com/christchurch-l1013/christchurch-tranzalpine-train-journey-t394220/), considered one of the most scenic rail journeys in the world. The train leaves Christchurch early, crosses the Canterbury plains and heads into the gorges of the Waimakariri River until it reaches the alpine heart of the country in the village of **Arthur's Pass**.

If you base yourself in the village or go by car, don't miss the walk to *Devil's Punchbowl*, a spectacular 131-meter waterfall surrounded by native forest. Keep your eyes open: you're very likely to run into the *Kea*, the world's only alpine parrot, known for being extremely curious (and for trying to bite car tires!).

## The International Antarctic Centre

Next to the international airport, the **International Antarctic Centre** is a fascinating interactive theme and science park. Christchurch has historically been one of the five official gateways to Antarctica (the United States and New Zealand missions depart from here). You can experience a real polar storm inside an ice dome at below-zero temperatures, ride in an original *Hagglund* all-terrain amphibious vehicle, and meet the little blue penguins at their rescue center.

## Practical info for staying in Christchurch

### Where to base yourself (recommended accommodation)

If you're arriving on a Working Holiday visa or planning your departure from the South Island abroad, staying in the city center will save you a lot of money on daily transport:

* **For backpackers / Working Holiday:** I recommend front-line hostels in the center like **Urbanz** (with excellent common areas) or the original **Jailhouse Accommodation** (a historic prison, remodeled and super cozy).
* **LyLo capsules:** If you want something modern, functional and hyper-comfortable, [LyLo Christchurch](https://www.booking.com/hotel/nz/jucy-snooze-christchurch.html) is one of the most popular options in the country, with private capsule-style cabins that let you sleep with total privacy at a super backpacker-friendly price.
* **For independent travelers:** If you prefer more privacy without losing the strategic location, you can look for [central hotels in Christchurch on Booking](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.es.html?city=-1508201) to be a few steps from the trams and get around entirely on foot.

### Transport and connectivity

* **From the airport to the center:** The regular bus (line 3 or *Purple Line*) takes you straight to the *Bus Interchange downtown* for just a few dollars. You can also take an Uber or an authorized taxi at the terminal exit.
* **Getting around the city:** Walking is the nicest option, but if you're in a hurry, rental electric scooters (like *Lime* or *Beam*) are the fastest way to get from the Botanic Gardens to the Arts Centre in a matter of minutes.

> **📢 Forewarned is forearmed!** Visa requirements and tourist pass prices in New Zealand change; I recommend always checking the official information before you travel.
