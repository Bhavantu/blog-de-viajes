Many of us who travel to New Zealand do it with the dream of living an epic *roadtrip*. We made it happen, and today we're sharing up-to-date information about our trip along the untamed west coast of the South Island.

The backbone of the trip is the legendary Route 6, but we took a detour on the recommendation of a local musician and added the magical Golden Bay peninsula via State Highway 60 (SH60). In this article you'll find an optimized 12-day route that takes you through golden-sand beaches, prehistoric forests in Abel Tasman National Park, mountain passes with incredible views and glaciers, all the way to Milford Sound, the deepest fjords on the planet.

> 📊 **ROADTRIP FACT SHEET (SUMMARY)**
>
> * **Total duration:** 12 days recommended.
> * **Estimated distance:** ~1,200 km (net driving: 20-22 hours spread out).
> * **Start and end point:** Picton to Milford Sound (South Island).
> * **Main roads:** State Highway 6 (Route 6) and State Highway 60 (SH60).
> * **Recommended vehicle:** Campervan or a vehicle with *Self-Contained* certification to make the most of the free campsites. Rent your camper here
>
>   Or a car with a suitable engine (minimum 1.8L - 2.0L), basically with enough power to climb the mountain passes comfortably (ideal if you choose to do the route with the [comfort of hotels.](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.es.html?ss=South+Island+%28Isla+Sur%29%2C+Nueva+Zelanda&ssne=Nueva+Zelanda&ssne_untouched=Nueva+Zelanda&efdco=1&label=nz-WCnuW9tzrM80NkDgoIXbNAS379601445588%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-357665074748%3Alp9072522%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Appccp%3DUmFuZG9tSVYkc2RlIyh9YfpWGnRw6lOGZNq08ntPlxk&aid=1610688&lang=es&sb=1&src_elem=sb&src=country&dest_id=1059&dest_type=region&ac_position=0&ac_click_type=b&ac_langcode=es&ac_suggestion_list_length=5&search_selected=true&search_pageview_id=e71f327af04803f5&ac_meta=GhBlNzFmMzI3YWYwNDgwM2Y1IAAoATICZXM6F051ZXZhIFplbGFuZGEgaXNsYSBzdXIg&checkin=2026-10-14&checkout=2026-10-15&ltfd=%3A%3A10-2026%3A1%3A&group_adults=2&no_rooms=1&group_children=0&sb_lp=1&selected_currency=USD#map_opened))
> * **Best time:** November to April (summer / autumn).

This quick guide gives you a north-to-south overview with iconic stops, driving-time references, safety alerts based on our experience and practical tips so you can enjoy the route stress-free.

## 1. Day 1: From Picton to Nelson along Queen Charlotte Drive (Route 6)

If you're coming from the North Island like us, the ferry drops you in Picton. After a stroll along the waterfront, you can head straight to Nelson along the beautiful Queen Charlotte Drive.

* **Key stretch:** 140 km (about 2 hours of net driving).
* **Must-sees on the way:**

  * **Pelorus Bridge:** A short 15-minute trail among giant trees over the Rai river. Perfect for stretching your legs.
  * **Cable Bay:** A beautiful detour where the mountain meets a gorgeous pebble beach.
* **Where to sleep:** Nelson has excellent Holiday Parks that you can find on the CamperMate app for *self-contained* vehicles. If you take the overnight ferry, as we did, there are also several *free camping* spots near the Picton area with mountain views.

## 2. Days 2 to 4: The SH60 detour to Golden Bay and Abel Tasman

We followed the recommendation of the local musician from Fat Eddie's and veered northeast to the Golden Bay peninsula. To do this, we temporarily left Route 6 and took State Highway 60 (SH60).

### 📍 SH60 route index (Nelson to Collingwood)

* **Stretch 1 (Nelson to Motueka):** 50 km of flat road through fruit orchards.
* **Stretch 2 (Motueka to Kaiteriteri):** 13 km coastal detour to the most golden-sand beach in the region. Highly recommended.
* **Stretch 3 (Motueka to Takaka):** 48 km crossing the Takaka Hill pass (the SH60 reaches 791 meters in height).
* **Stretch 4 (Takaka to Collingwood):** 28 km of relaxed driving through the bay's valley, between green hills and deserted beaches.

### 🌟 Must-sees on this stretch

* **Kaiteriteri Beach:** Golden sand and turquoise water before climbing the mountain.
* **Abel Tasman National Park:** Trails among giant ferns and incredible views of the sea. [You can camp by booking ahead on the DOC website](https://www.doc.govt.nz/parks-and-recreation/places-to-go/nelson-tasman/places/abel-tasman-national-park/things-to-do/tracks/abel-tasman-coast-track/). You can go overland or [from the shores of Kaiteriteri Beach on a kayak excursion.](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/marahau-l142416/marahau-abel-tasman-kayaks-kayak-y-paseo-t791969/?ranking_uuid=e71e9b85-2f6f-4956-a165-ae3a128bf8ba)
* **Te Waikoropupū Springs:** The most sacred and crystal-clear freshwater springs in the country.
* **Collingwood:** Endless green landscapes that look like a Windows wallpaper.
* **Wharariki Beach:** A wild, windy coast guarded by the iconic giant rock arches rising out of the sea.

> ⚠️ **SAFETY ALERT ON THE SH60** 
> The climb and descent of Takaka Hill have sharp cliff-edge bends. 2026 update: The roadworks are finished and the whole road is open in excellent condition. Even so, if you drive a big campervan, keep in mind that you need to climb in low gears and courteously use the *passing bays* to let locals through.

**Note: The SH60 is a dead end, meaning that to get back on Route 6 and head south you have to retrace your steps and cross the mountain again. We recommend setting aside at least 3 full days to enjoy it without rushing, or you can choose to stay on Route 6 only and go straight to point 5. Another option is to [see Golden Bay on a 1-day tour from Nelson](https://www.getyourguide.com/es-es/nelson-nueva-zelanda-l1758/nelson-excursion-de-un-dia-a-golden-bay-t1009283/?ranking_uuid=af1abcb7-94e3-4c03-bc38-96c9875e1769&q=Parque+Nacional+Abel+Tasman+en+sup) visiting the area's iconic spots.**

## 3. Day 5: From Golden Bay to Punakaiki (Pancake Rocks) along Route 6

<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-derecha"><img src="/img/diario/murchison-.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption"></span></div>

We crossed Takaka Hill again on the SH60, reconnected with Route 6 heading south and passed through the town of Murchison (ideal for filling up on fuel and resting) until we reached the wild Tasman Sea. We chose to spend the afternoon and sleep there, in my opinion a place of calm and natural beauty, a true hidden gem. 

* **Key stretch:** 265 km (approximately 3.5 hours of driving).
* **Must-sees on the route:**

  * **Buller Gorge:** The longest suspension bridge in New Zealand.
  * **Punakaiki (Pancake Rocks):** Limestone formations that look like piles of giant pancakes. If you go at high tide, you'll see the blowholes shoot seawater meters into the air.

## 4. Day 6: From Punakaiki to Franz Josef (Glacier Region)

Driving south, we reached the wet glacier region, where the vegetation and landscapes change completely (and so does the cold, especially at dawn).

* **Key stretch:** 220 km (about 3 hours of driving).
* **Must-sees on the way:**

  * **Hokitika Gorge:** A short trail that takes you to a suspension bridge over a river of a turquoise color that's simply unmissable. The walk is quick and in season it's a very touristy area.

## 5. Days 7 and 8: Glaciers, Mirror Lakes and the Haast Pass to Wanaka

Transition days between the humid tropical rainforest and the imposing Southern Alps.

* **Key stretch:** 285 km spread over two days to enjoy the alpine scenery.
* **Points of interest on this route:** 

  * **Lake Matheson:** The sunrise walk to see the Mount Cook peaks reflected in the water.
  * **Franz Josef & Fox Glacier:** Free viewpoints to see the tongue of the glaciers, or you can also take [the helicopter excursions to land on the glacier.](https://www.booking.com/attractions/nz/prd6drsc5dzp-franz-josef-glacier-helicopter-flight-with-snow-landing.es.html?selected_currency=USD&source=searchresults-product-card&start_date=2026-12-17&end_date=2026-12-17&aid=2427663&label=milford-sound-attractions-D6Kuaf7TYN8%2AtM59pAzuIwS717216742695%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-2282772141770%3Alp9227990%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Acid21786396167%3Aagid172490118841&date=2026-12-17&ufi=-1509737&timeslot=ATS-PRd6DRSc5Dzp-202612170800-nullnull&ticket_type=OFi6wVFiukZc)
  * **Blue Pools:** Natural pools of turquoise, crystal-clear meltwater in Makarora.

## 6. Day 9: From Wanaka to Queenstown along Crown Range Road

We crossed from a lake town to the world capital of adventure sports.

* **Key stretch:** 70 km along Crown Range Road (the highest paved road in the country, with spectacular views) or 110 km through the Cromwell valley.
* **Must-sees on the way:**

  * **That Wanaka Tree:** The famous lone tree standing in Lake Wanaka. The town is also beautiful; we were lucky enough to be there on December 31st and the atmosphere was one of a kind: food fair, live music and fireworks. A beautiful experience for spending New Year's Eve far from home but in good company.

> 💡 **TIP FOR SLEEPING IN WANAKA**: Since it's a very touristy area in season, you need to get to the *free campings* very early or book ahead at the Holiday Parks. If you park on the street in restricted areas, the fine is considerable. You can also find [hotels in Wanaka to spend the night.](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.es.html?ss=Wanaka&ssne=Wanaka&ssne_untouched=Wanaka&theme_id=8&efdco=1&label=New_Spanish_ES_ROW_5496343705-IRXF**fXkjlT1Jfkfsb2mAS637942155485%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atidsa-55482331735%3Alp9227990%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Aag5496343705%3Acmp108540265&sid=b79c37fc00fe5273ba01510bca8165bb&aid=318615&lang=es&sb=1&src_elem=sb&src=city&dest_id=-1516080&dest_type=city&checkin=2026-12-27&checkout=2026-12-28&group_adults=2&no_rooms=1&group_children=0&sb_lp=1&chal_t=1783608741051&force_referer=https%3A%2F%2Fblogcito.com%2F)

## 7. Days 10 to 12: From Queenstown to Milford Sound

The grand finale. The scenic road from Te Anau to Milford Sound is a beautiful, enjoyable road trip the whole way.

**Day 10 - Queenstown:** A vibrant city. Restaurants, shops, street artists and incredible views of the lake and the mountains. A walk through the center at sunset, a good meal and some rest are highly recommended. That said, we didn't find many Holiday Parks really close by, so you either have to keep going toward the outskirts or [stay in a hotel in Queenstown](https://www.booking.com/searchresults.es.html?ss=Queenstown&ssne=Queenstown&ssne_untouched=Queenstown) and rest up for the next day's adventure at Milford Sound.

* **Key stretch:** 290 km (allow at least 4 hours one way because of the difficult terrain and photo stops).
* **Must-sees on the way:**

  * **Lake Te Anau:** A giant lake surrounded by fjords, an easy flat walk with amazing views.
  * **Homer Tunnel:** An impressive feat of engineering carved by hand into the living rock of the mountain. Stop when you come out of the tunnel: spectacular lookout.
  * **Lake Marian Track:** A trail we loved. It's super rugged, takes about 3 hours (round trip) and starts by crossing a suspension bridge over the Marian River. The first 10-15 minutes are flat up to the waterfalls, but after that the path becomes a real adventure. The terrain is uneven, and you have to make your way dodging giant roots of century-old trees, rocks and muddy patches. It's a very entertaining technical trek that forces you to be present until you reach a beautiful alpine lake.
  * **[Milford Sound:](https://www.booking.com/attractions/nz/prh1d08irngk-2-hour-milford-sound-scenic-cruise.es.html?aid=2427663&label=milford-sound-attractions-D6Kuaf7TYN8%2AtM59pAzuIwS717216742695%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-2282772141770%3Alp9227990%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Acid21786396167%3Aagid172490118841&source=searchresults-product-card&ufi=900052740&date=2026-07-08&timeslot=ATS-PRH1D08irNgk-202607081100-nullnull&ticket_type=OFI0NG3wxIFp)** Definitely one of my favorites and a must-see in New Zealand (and probably for a lot of people). Meltwater waterfalls falling down the rock fjords into the dark, deep waters. You can walk the nearby trails, sail its fjords and even visit it by helicopter. The road goes under a tunnel and it's like being teleported into a movie, with must-see lookouts at every kilometer.

🚢 **Sailing the fjords:** One of the best ways to experience Milford Sound is by sailing its waters. Morning cruise departures are usually cheaper and less crowded. Secure your ticket by booking [your Milford Sound cruise here](https://www.booking.com/attractions/nz/prh1d08irngk-2-hour-milford-sound-scenic-cruise.es.html?aid=2427663&label=milford-sound-attractions-D6Kuaf7TYN8%2AtM59pAzuIwS717216742695%3Apl%3Ata%3Ap1%3Ap2%3Aac%3Aap%3Aneg%3Afi%3Atikwd-2282772141770%3Alp9227990%3Ali%3Adec%3Adm%3Acid21786396167%3Aagid172490118841&source=searchresults-product-card&ufi=900052740&date=2026-07-08&timeslot=ATS-PRH1D08irNgk-202607081100-nullnull&ticket_type=OFI0NG3wxIFp).

### What are the freedom camping rules in New Zealand?

They are strict. If you camp in restricted areas without the official *self-contained* sticker (which certifies your own toilet in the van), you risk immediate fines of more than $400 NZD. We recommend checking the alerts on the CamperMate app or at DOC visitor centres.

### Where can I check the official status of the roads?

The weather on the West Coast is unpredictable and rockfalls are common. Before tackling mountain stretches like Takaka Hill or the road to Milford Sound, check the [NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi website](https://www.nzta.govt.nz) or look at our regular updates at https://blogcito.com/en/journal/.
