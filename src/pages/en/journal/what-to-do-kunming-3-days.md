We spent 3 days here in October 2026, staying at the Yunyiduo Hotel, next to the Dashuying metro station (about 4 or 5 km from Green Lake Park). Kunming, the capital of Yunnan, is the best gateway to this province in southwest China. It is a relaxed city with a mild climate, parks full of people enjoying themselves, tea houses and a very particular cuisine. Our main piece of advice is to use Kunming as a 2 or 3 day base before visiting Dali, Lijiang and Shangri-La; we decided to leave those for another trip.
This guide is part of Traveling Independently in China: a 20-day itinerary from south to center. Kunming was our second stop, after Nanning and before Chengdu.

## Our Experience in Kunming

We arrived from Nanning and stayed 3 nights at the Yunyiduo Hotel (the usual rate is around 20 USD per night; we paid more because our stay coincided with Golden Week), on Dongfeng East Road, a few steps from the Dashuying metro station. It is not right next to Green Lake Park (it is about 4 or 5 km away), but with the metro or a DiDi we could get to the parks, the tea houses and Yuantong Temple. If you prefer to stay near the lake, a good option is the Orange Hotel (Kunming Green Lake), from about 40 USD per night. Kunming felt like one of the most relaxed cities of the trip: nobody seems to be in a hurry, people are open to a chat and the afternoons are spent outdoors, in the parks.
[From Kunming we took the high-speed train to Chengdu, the city of the pandas](https://www.trip.com/t/W63vDRcH0X2).

## Why Yunnan: Ethnic Diversity

Yunnan is the province with the greatest ethnic diversity in China. Around 25 minorities live here, making up about a third of the population (around 33%). The Dai, Bai, Yi, Naxi, Hani, Miao or Tibetans keep their own languages, costumes, festivals, architecture and cuisine.
That mix shows in the wooden houses of Lijiang, the white facades of Dali, the Tibetan monasteries of the north and the markets. We did not get to those cities on this trip (below we explain why), but Kunming works as a first contact: there is an entire park dedicated to the province's ethnic groups, and daily life already feels different from Nanning or Chengdu.

## When to Go to Kunming

Kunming is nicknamed the "City of Eternal Spring" because of its altitude (about 1,900 meters) and its latitude: summers are mild, winters are gentle and there are flowers almost all year round. There are two distinct seasons, one dry and one rainy.
| Season | What to expect |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Spring (March to May) | One of the best times. Days of around 20 °C, little rain and a city full of flowers. |
| Summer (June to August) | Pleasant, with highs of 24 to 25 °C and lows around 16 °C, but it is the rainy season. |
| Autumn (September to November) | When we went (October). Clear skies and temperatures of about 13 to 21 °C. September still has some rainy days. |
| Winter (December to February) | Dry and sunny, but cool: highs of about 15 °C and lows of 3 to 5 °C. There are fewer tourists. Gulls arrive at the city's lakes (from November to March). |

If you can choose, we recommend March to May or October and November. Dress in layers: mornings can be warm and nights cool. In our opinion, the October temperature was pretty much ideal and felt like spring.

## What to See in Kunming

### 1. Yunnan Ethnic Village (the best introduction to the province)

The Yunnan Ethnic Village covers 89 hectares next to Dianchi Lake, in the southwest of the city, and brings together the architecture, costumes and traditions of 25 ethnic groups. It opened in 1992 and each village shows full-scale houses, food, rituals and celebrations of its community, with music and dance shows included in the ticket.

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/parque-de-las-etnias-yunnan.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Yunnan Ethnic Village</span></div>

An anecdote: I wanted to dress up as one of the ethnic groups and asked around in several places, but in the end I did not do it. Later, in Furongzhen, I did get up the courage to wear traditional Miao clothing: we tell the story in our [Furongzhen guide](/diario/furongzhen-que-ver/).

Still, to be honest: it is a site designed for visitors and not the real life of those communities. We took it as a first introduction and, as such, we found it an interesting way into the province's cultures. The shows inside the park are very entertaining and, in general, they end up inviting the audience to dance in a big circle.

* Location: 1310 Dianchi Road, Xishan District, Kunming.
* Admission: 90 CNY (about USD 13.41 or EUR 12.06) at the gate. [Book the experience with transfer included from the city center here.](https://www.trip.com/travel-guide/attraction/kunming/yunnan-nationalities-village-75906?poiType=3&curr=USD&locale=en-XX&scene=ticket) The night show is paid separately.
* Hours: from 9:00 to 18:00, with last entry around 16:30. It is quite large, so ideally you should dedicate a whole day to it.
* How to get there: there is no metro station at the entrance. We left from the hotel, next to Dashuying station (lines 3 and 4), took the metro and walked the last stretch: it did not feel heavy. On the way back, already tired, we took a DiDi. If you do not want to walk, you can combine the metro with one of the buses that stop at the Yunnan Ethnic Village station (for example the 24, the 44 or the 73), or go straight by DiDi. Plan the route on Amap or Baidu Maps, which work better than Google Maps in China.
* Best time: in the morning, to enjoy it without the heat, or in the afternoon, after noon, to see the shows.
* Pros: you can see it in one day, there is a lot of information about each culture in English and the shows are included.
* Cons: it is a tourist site; there is no metro at the entrance (you have to walk a stretch, take a bus or a DiDi), and show times change with the season.
* Tip: when you enter, ask for the map and check the day's shows.

### 2. Green Lake Park, Cuihu (a residential park)

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/green-lake-park.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Green Lake Park (Cuihu), in the center of Kunming</span></div>

Green Lake Park (翠湖, Cuìhú) is right in the center and locals call it the "jewel of Kunming". It is made up of four small lakes joined by traditional-style bridges, surrounded by a street with restaurants and tea houses. The Yunnan University and Yuantong Temple are nearby. What we liked most is that it is a park where you also run into the people who live there: there are tea houses all around, shade to walk in and lots of people taking photos in the traditional costumes of the Han or Tujia ethnic groups. We combined it with the temple visit: we walked around the park and then decided to have something to drink in a tea house across from the park.

* Admission: free.
* Hours: open 24 hours.
* Best time: in the afternoon, when the weather is pleasant, or in the morning.
* Tip: take the chance to sit in one of the tea houses by the lake.

### 3. Yuantong Temple (the oldest and largest in Kunming)

It is the oldest and largest Buddhist temple in the city, known for its central pond and its architecture surrounded by vegetation. It is one of the oldest and most important Buddhist centers in Yunnan, with more than 1,200 years of history. Three major schools of Buddhism coexist in this temple.

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/yuantong-templo-budista.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Yuantong Temple in Kunming</span></div>

Its main line is Chinese Mahāyāna, dedicated mainly to Guanyin (the goddess of compassion), where its incense altars and its central pond stand out. At the same time it coexists with Theravada, present in a Thai-style pavilion with a bronze Buddha statue given by Thailand, and with Tibetan Vajrayana, which brings its tantric details and the visual essence of the mountain areas of the region. A unique mix that sums up the cultural richness of Yunnan.

* Hours: from 8:00 to 17:00. The visit takes about an hour and a half.
* Admission: 6 CNY per person (about USD 0.89 or EUR 0.80), incense included.
* How to get there: about a 10-minute walk from Green Lake Park.
* Best time: at opening, to start the day here.

### 4. Tea Houses and Pu'er (the Rhythm of Kunming)

Yunnan is tea country and pu'er, famous throughout China, comes from this province. In Kunming it is enjoyed slowly: in the tea houses around the parks, on terraces under the trees or in the markets where tea cakes are sold. Here nobody seems to be in a hurry, and the afternoon goes by with a cup of tea and a chat.

### 5. Dounan Flower Market

One of the most popular spots in Kunming is the Dounan fresh flower market, considered the largest in Asia: a spectacle of color where millions of flowers are sold every day. Around 7 in the evening the discounts begin and people come to buy and stroll along the aisles full of orchids and roses, at very low prices.

<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/mercado-de-flores-de-dounan-kunming.webp" alt="Imagen de bitácora"><span class="img-caption">Dounan Flower Market, the largest flower market in Asia</span></div>

Both for getting to know it and for a walk it is an interesting experience, and in the surrounding area there is also a fair where you can take photos with the classic flower crowns. Also, if your visit coincides with the winter months, you can go to the shore of nearby Dianchi Lake, where it is a tradition to welcome the red-billed gulls that migrate every year.
How to get there: it is in the Chenggong district, about 18 km from the center. You can go by metro (the nearest stations are Dounan and Jingui Street) or by DiDi, about 40 minutes from the center. Plan the route on Amap or Baidu Maps before leaving.

## What to Eat in Kunming

Yunnan cuisine is one of the most varied and distinctive in China, with mountain ingredients, herbs, flowers and fresh, sour and spicy flavors. These are the typical dishes of the region:
| Dish | What it is |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "Crossing the bridge" noodles (过桥米线) | A very hot broth to which rice noodles, meat and vegetables are added at the table. |
| Wild mushrooms | In season, in soup or sautéed, in restaurants and markets. They are also found in vegetarian hot pot. |
| Flower cakes | Sweets filled with petals, a classic Kunming pastry. |

Local markets let you try them for little money. We paid about 18 CNY (about USD 2.68 or EUR 2.41) outside the Ethnic Village Park. If you have dietary restrictions, always ask about the ingredients: spiciness and meat broths are common.
You can also try the local food at Kunming Old Street and at the Nanqiang Night Market, full of traditional houses from the Qing era and the Republic, with street food stalls.

## What We Liked, What We Didn't and Is It Worth It?

What we liked: the weather, the relaxed pace, the parks full of life and the tea.
What we didn't like: the main thing that challenged us most in China was finding vegetarian food. Even so, with the translator we managed to get some dishes adapted and, searching on Google, we found a few Buddhist canteens and vegetarian restaurants.
Is it worth it? Yes, as a 2 or 3 day break on a long trip through China or as a gateway to Yunnan. If you only have a couple of days and want to see monuments or tech cities, you will probably prefer another city.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days do you need in Kunming?**

2 or 3 days is enough for the parks, the temple, the tea houses and the Ethnic Village. We stayed 3 nights.

**What is the best time to travel to Kunming?**

From March to May and in October and November, with mild weather and little rain. In summer it rains more and in winter it gets cool at night.

**Is the Yunnan Ethnic Village worth it?**

Yes, if you take it as a first introduction to the province's diversity and not as the real life of the communities. Admission costs 90 CNY.

**How do I get to the Ethnic Village from the center?**

By metro and walking the last stretch, as we did (it does not feel heavy), or combining the metro with one of the buses that stop at the Yunnan Ethnic Village station (for example the 24, the 44 or the 73). You can also go by DiDi, which is what we used to come back. Rental bikes can only be used with a local phone number, so they are not an option for tourists unless you buy one and set it up on WeChat.

**Is Kunming expensive?**

No. A plate of noodles or local food costs around 25 CNY (about USD 3.73) and the markets let you eat at affordable prices.

**Can you go to Dali and Lijiang from Kunming?**

Yes, there is a high-speed train. We did not go on this trip, so we cannot tell you what it is like in person.

**Who do we recommend it for?**

* Backpackers and slow travelers: ideal as a pause between cities.
* Couples: parks, tea houses and quiet sunsets.
* Families: the Ethnic Village works well with kids, and they can even dress up.
