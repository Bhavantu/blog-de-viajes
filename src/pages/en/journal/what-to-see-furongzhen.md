It exists, it's called Furongzhen and it's well worth it, especially if you stay the night.** We spent 2 nights and 3 days in this town hanging over a waterfall, in Hunan, during our 20-day route through China in October 2026, and we stayed at Jianghu, a guesthouse inside the old town's grounds (the area that requires a ticket). By day it's a town of cobbled streets and Tujia houses on stilts; at night, when the waterfall lights up, it feels like a different place. We also ate the famous mi doufu at the very spot where the town's film was shot. In this guide we tell you how to get there from Zhangjiajie or Fenghuang, how much the ticket costs, what to see and when, and which shows you can't miss. One tip before we start: no cars are allowed inside the town, so travel light.

## A Town Hanging Over a Waterfall


Furongzhen is a town more than 2,000 years old, set on a cliff in western Hunan. By day it's a town of cobbled streets, Tujia houses on wooden stilts and cultural shows by the Tujia people. At nightfall the lights come on, and the waterfall, which drops right in the middle of town, lights up and creates one of its most iconic images; honestly, the scene looks like something out of a fairy tale.


<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/furongzhen-pueblo-sobre-la-cascada.webp" alt="The town of Furongzhen above the waterfall, by day" width="1920" height="1280" loading="lazy"><span class="img-caption">The town of Furongzhen above the waterfall, by day</span></div>


The town lies halfway between Zhangjiajie and Fenghuang. Historically it was inhabited by two of China's ethnic minorities, the Tujia and the Miao. It was the first seat of the Tusi chieftains of Xizhou, the Peng family, a local Tujia dynasty that ruled the region for more than 800 years, from the 10th century until 1729. In 1135 they moved their seat to Laosicheng, but the town kept the name Wangcun, "the king's village". From that era remain the Tusi Palace, the chieftains' summer residence on the cliff, and the Xizhou Bronze Pillar, cast around the year 940 to record a treaty between the King of Chu and the local chieftain.


**The name change and the film that made it famous:**


For most of its history it was called Wangcun (王村). In 1986 *Hibiscus Town* (*Furong Zhen*, 《芙蓉镇》) was shot here, a film by Xie Jin based on Gu Hua's novel, which tells the story of a young woman who sells rice tofu, the town's signature dish, in the middle of the Cultural Revolution. The film made the town famous all over China and, years later, it officially adopted the name Furong.


Today it works as a tourist site with an entrance ticket: it's an ethical and responsible way to get closer to the local culture. The ticket is valid for three days and includes the historic center, the waterfall viewpoints and the Tujia dance and music shows. We go into more detail further down.


## Quick Summary


The ticket costs 98 CNY (USD 14.98 or about EUR 13). You can spend the night (we recommend it) or visit it as a day trip. It isn't very big, so you can walk around it in a few hours, but in the late afternoon and evening it becomes magical with its colored lights.

## Our Experience in Furongzhen


When we arrived at the station, someone from our guesthouse was waiting for us; we had arranged it through the app. The grounds are inside the town, so they drove us to the entrance, where we showed our entry QR code and they scanned our passports. Then the car dropped us off on the hotel's street and, once we had settled in, we started exploring the town.

The waterfall looked amazing; we started walking around at about 4 in the afternoon in beautiful light, we went along the walkway behind it several times, and there were quite a few people, but it was still enjoyable. That afternoon I took the chance to fulfil the classic China dream and went to one of the stalls where they do your makeup and dress you in the traditional clothing of the Miao people. So when night fell, besides enjoying the light show all over town, we took photos at several of the lit-up spots, which I share below.

Since we were staying two nights, on the first day we took the chance to walk around calmly and try the famous rice tofu, "mi doufu". We went to the most iconic spot in town, a small stall where the film "Hibiscus Town" was shot. When we got there we found a big group of people who kindly invited us to eat and chat with them. They turned out to be the local police, who had just finished their shift; we talked, laughed and also chatted with the owners, who, laughing, showed us photos of some iconic moments from the film and even one of Xi Jinping, the president of China. After 22:00 the town starts to empty and the viewpoints clear out.


<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/furongzhen-mesa-compartida-mi-doufu.webp" alt="With the local police of Furongzhen, who invited us to their table at the mi doufu shop" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy"><span class="img-caption">With the local police of Furongzhen, who invited us to their table at the mi doufu shop</span></div>


Mornings are quiet too, and we spent the second day watching the performances at the different times, which you can check at the tourist point at the entrance. One of our favorites was the reenactment of the run-up to a Tujia wedding on the Tuwang Bridge, at around 14:30, which shows the bride's grief at leaving her home and family with a very particular song, but also the joy of the engagement. It ends in a very lively way and they hand out little gifts.


The show we highlight above all is the night one. We went quite early: when the previous show, "the Bizika carnival", ended, a long line formed, but we got in first and found a good spot in the indoor amphitheater. If you're short on time, one well-planned night is enough.

## What to See in Furongzhen: Approximate Times


This is how we organized the day. Times are approximate and change with the season: check them at the entrance on the day you go.


* **From 9:00.** A good time to walk: everything is very quiet and from 9:00 there are shows in different parts of the town.
* **Suggested walks.** Baishou Hall, the Bronze Pillar park, Tuwang Bridge, the Tusi Palace, the Tujia houses on stilts and Wuli Street (see the order of the route below).
* **Around 14:30.** The reenactment of the run-up to a wedding on the bridge (one of our favorites).
* **16:30 to 17:30.** Good light to look at the town from the viewpoint, from the Youshui River or over a drink at one of the bars with a waterfall view.
* **18:30.** Early dinner on Wuli Street.
* **From 19:00.** The main waterfall viewpoint and the lights coming on.
* **19:30.** "The Carnival Party" (狂欢派对), the first part of "Flower in Full Bloom: the Bizika Carnival", at Baishou Hall (摆手堂, next to the entrance): an open-air show with dances, rituals and traditional drums. As soon as it ends, at 20:00, we recommend going straight to line up for the final show.
* **20:30, at the Yinan Theater (翼南剧场):** "Affection in Youshui" (情满酉水), the second part and the town's big performance, with Tujia dance and music in an indoor theater.
* **After the show.** The walkway behind the waterfall and the streets with the lanterns lit, with fewer people.


### Iconic Spots and Route


The map of the grounds suggests a one-way walk, almost all downhill, from the east gate to the river pier: Gate and visitor center → Baishou Hall → Bronze Pillar park → Tuwang Bridge → Tusi Palace → Tingtao Street and Yinan Square → waterfall viewpoint → waterfall and the walkway behind it → pier → Wuli Street. This is a suggested route, and if you're staying inside the grounds you'll probably have to adapt it to your starting point.

* **Best time:** in the morning and at night, when there's hardly anyone around. In the afternoon it gets crowded.
* **What we experienced:** the waterfall looked amazing from the viewpoint and the walkway is fun. We went behind it several times and took the chance to cool off. It splashes!

* **What it is:** a covered wooden bridge with red lanterns, one of the best spots for photos, especially at dusk.
* **What we experienced:** this is where we saw "The Bride's Lament", the run-up to a Tujia wedding, at around 14:30.


### 3. Wuli Street and the Tujia Houses on Stilts


<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/furongzhen-calles-faroles.webp" alt="One of the streets of Furongzhen's old town, with lanterns and restaurants" width="1280" height="1920" loading="lazy"><span class="img-caption">One of the streets of Furongzhen's old town, with lanterns and restaurants</span></div>


* **What it is:** the stone-slab street (五里石板街) with wooden houses on stilts. Restaurants with views of the waterfall or the river.
* **Best time:** in the afternoon, at dusk.


### 4. Tusi Palace (土王行宫)


* **What it is:** the summer palace of the Tusi chieftains, the Peng family that ruled the region for more than 800 years. It's also called the Youyang Palace (酉阳宫). It sits on the edge of the cliff, next to the waterfall: according to travel guides, the chieftains chose the spot because in summer it's the coolest place in town.
* **What it's like:** a wooden building in the style of traditional Tujia halls, with the beams joined by wooden joints. From there you can see the waterfall and the town, and today it's one of the main stops on the route.
* **What we experienced:** we walked past the palace, but we don't have photos of the inside.


### 5. Xizhou Bronze Pillar Park and Baishou Hall


<div class="post-img-box img-mediano img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/furongzhen-pilar-de-bronce-xizhou-estatuas.webp" alt="The Xizhou Bronze Pillar and the treaty statues, in Furongzhen" width="1616" height="1920" loading="lazy"><span class="img-caption">The Xizhou Bronze Pillar and the treaty statues, in Furongzhen</span></div>


* **What it is:** the pillar records the treaty of the year 940, and the hall is the Tujia ceremonial space where some of the daytime shows are held; there are also statues representing the treaty between the leaders of the Tujia people and the ancient kingdom of Chu in 940 AD. The pillar sealed the alliance by setting clear borders and guaranteeing the autonomy and trade of the native peoples, putting an end to armed conflict in the Xiangxi mountains (this is a short summary; if you're interested, we recommend reading more).


## Main Shows in Furongzhen


The shows are included in the ticket and there are several throughout the day, such as "The Bride's Lament", the acrobatics and knife show or folk dances. You can come across them during your walk because they take place in public spaces, or you can check the times on the board at the information center when you go in.

The main ones are in the late afternoon and evening, and these stand out:


### The Lit-Up Waterfall, at 19:00


Around 19:00 the town's lights come on and the central waterfall lights up. It's a great moment to watch the sunset from the main viewpoint. Afterwards you can go down to the walkway behind the waterfall and stroll the streets with the lanterns lit. At night, around 22:00, the town starts to empty, so it's also a great time to enjoy the quiet.


<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/furongzhen-cascada-noche-pueblo-iluminado.webp" alt="The Furongzhen waterfall lit up at night, with the town glowing above" width="1920" height="1280" loading="lazy"><span class="img-caption">The Furongzhen waterfall lit up at night, with the town glowing above</span></div>


### The Final Show at the Yinan Theater


The main evening performance is at 20:30 at the Yinan Theater (翼南剧场). It's the second part of "Flower in Full Bloom: the Bizika Carnival" (《花开芙蓉·毕兹卡的狂欢》), the town's night show: the first part, "The Carnival Party" (狂欢派对), is at 19:30, outdoors, at Baishou Hall (摆手堂). The 20:30 one is called "Affection in Youshui" (情满酉水), as the town's sign translates it, after the river that runs through Furongzhen.


<div class="post-img-box img-completo img-centro"><img src="/img/diario/furongzhen-show-nocturno-novios.webp" alt="A scene from &quot;Affection in Youshui&quot;, the 20:30 show at the Yinan Theater in Furongzhen" width="1920" height="1280" loading="lazy"><span class="img-caption">A scene from "Affection in Youshui", the 20:30 show at the Yinan Theater in Furongzhen</span></div>


Our tip: get there early. When the open-air dance and drum show at Baishou Hall ends, a line forms to get in. You can see well from anywhere because it's an amphitheater, but if there are a lot of tour groups you risk not getting a seat.


## How to Get to Furongzhen from Zhangjiajie or Fenghuang


The simplest option is the high-speed train. From Zhangjiajie it takes about 23 minutes and from Fenghuang, about 33.


* **[From Zhangjiajie.](https://www.trip.com/t/kajPzscm5X2)** You leave from Zhangjiajie West station (张家界西, the high-speed one). The trip to Furongzhen (芙蓉镇) takes about 23 minutes and costs 54 CNY (about USD 8, EUR 7) on Trip.com. There are more than ten departures a day, but check availability for your date.
* **[From Fenghuang.](https://www.trip.com/t/SJkDorxm5X2)** The train goes from Fenghuang Ancient Town station (凤凰古城) to Furongzhen in about 33 minutes, for 67 CNY (USD 9.94, about EUR 8.7) on Trip.com. There are several departures a day in the opposite direction; check which is the last one, at 19:45.
* **From the station to the town.** The station is about 10 or 15 minutes by car from the old town. According to Trip.com's transport guide, a taxi costs about 20 CNY (USD 3, EUR 2.6); we didn't take one because we were picked up. The most practical option is to arrange it with your accommodation: many will pick you up if you tell them your arrival time.
* Note: there are no cars inside the town, so keep that in mind for carrying your luggage. As for accommodation, we stayed at Jianghu, which is inside the grounds, on one of its outer streets, and they picked us up, so even though it doesn't have a direct view of the waterfall it was a great choice.

## Tujia Culture


The Tujia are one of China's largest minorities and live mainly in the Wuling Mountains. The town preserves the architecture (the houses on wooden stilts, anchored into the cliff) and the shows throughout the day. The palace you see along the route recalls the era when Wangcun was the seat of the Tusi chieftains. The town is still inhabited: according to the 2010 census, the municipality of Furong (which includes the old town and its surroundings) has 22,261 inhabitants.


## Where to Sleep and What to Eat


### Where to Stay in Furongzhen


There are two options: **inside the grounds** (views of the waterfall and the river, a magical atmosphere, but a bit more expensive and with stairs) or **outside, near the gate** (cheaper and easier with luggage). We stayed inside and we recommend it.


* [Jianghu](https://www.trip.com/t/DYY7oUko5X2) (our choice): we paid about 120 CNY (USD 18, EUR 16) per night. We stayed two nights, with lovely views of the town and the river. It's inside the grounds, on one of its outer streets: on that side the car dropped us off and, on the other, the hotel has direct access to the inside of the old town. We chose the Chan Yun Twin Room.
* [Tuwang Palace · Eight Halls](https://www.trip.com/t/LMlRvsdn5X2): about 389 CNY (USD 58, EUR 50). Views of the waterfall from the hotel, right in the central area of the grounds.

### What to Eat in Furongzhen


The restaurants line Wuli Street, many with views of the waterfall or the river. Hunan is known for its spicy dishes, and Tujia cuisine is dominated by smoked meats and combinations with wild mushrooms and herbs. Furongzhen's iconic dish is **rice tofu (mi doufu, 米豆腐)**. It isn't soy tofu: it's made with rice flour and served with seasonings in a broth; honestly, the flavor was quite similar to gnocchi without sauce.

#### Mi Doufu at the Film Location: Liu Xiaoqing (刘晓庆米豆腐店)


We ate at the place where *Hibiscus Town* was filmed. It's Liu Xiaoqing's mi doufu shop; she is the actress who plays the vendor in the 1986 film. Inside there were photos from the film and even one of Xi Jinping, the president of China. We ended up eating there as guests of the local police, laughing and swapping stories, but we tell that in our experience.


* **How to find it:** near a small stone arch, with the sign "芙蓉镇113号" (Furong Town No. 113), which is the filming location. According to the Hunan Ribao newspaper (2021), they sold more than 3,000 bowls a day.
* **Price:** the small bowl cost 8 CNY (about USD 1.20, EUR 1).
* **What to expect:** it's a simple place and a simple dish; you go more for the experience.


Are you vegetarian? In some of the town's shops we found fried rice with vegetables or rice noodles without broth.

## What We Liked, What We Didn't and Is It Worth It?

**What we liked:** the waterfall lit up at night and the walkway behind it, the run-up to the Tujia wedding on Tuwang Bridge, the night show "Flower in Full Bloom" and the mi doufu at the film location.

**What was hard:** finding our way. The grounds are quite a maze and we struggled to read the map to find the shows and the stops on the route. There were buildings and points of interest we couldn't reach: a guard told us that some can only be seen from outside and can't be visited.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Furongzhen


### How much is the ticket to Furongzhen?


The ticket costs 98 CNY (USD 14.98) per person on Trip.com and is valid for three days with re-entry. Teenagers and seniors pay less.


### How do you get to Furongzhen from Zhangjiajie?


By high-speed train from Zhangjiajie West to Furongzhen Station, about 23 minutes, for 54 CNY (about USD 8) on Trip.com. From the station it's 10 to 15 minutes by car to the town.


### How many days do you need in Furongzhen?


One well-planned night is enough: you arrive in the afternoon, see the lit-up waterfall and the show, and explore the town quietly in the morning. We stayed two. To see it during the day, about 3 hours is enough.


### What time does the Furongzhen waterfall light up?


Around 19:00 in October. Check at the entrance on the day you go.


### Are there daytime shows in Furongzhen?


Yes, there are several and they're included in the ticket. For example, we saw the run-up to a Tujia wedding on the bridge at around 14:30. Check the board at the information center, because times change.


### Where can you eat the mi doufu from the film in Furongzhen?


At Liu Xiaoqing's shop (刘晓庆米豆腐店), the one with the sign "芙蓉镇113号". There are many copies: make sure it says 113.


### Are cars allowed in the town of Furongzhen?


No, there are no cars inside the town. Suitcases are carried on foot, so it's best to travel light.


### Can you visit Furongzhen as a day trip from Zhangjiajie?


Yes, by train, but you miss the night, which is the best part of the town. Check the last train back: to Zhangjiajie West it leaves around 19:20; check it on the day you go.


### When is the best time to go to Furongzhen?


From March to November, with spring and autumn as the favorites. The waterfall drops a lot between December and February.


## Who Is Furongzhen For?


* **Couples and photographers:** ideal at night with the lit-up waterfall.
* **Slow travelers:** a quiet night between Zhangjiajie and Fenghuang.
* **Culture lovers:** the shows, Tujia history and the mi doufu shop.
