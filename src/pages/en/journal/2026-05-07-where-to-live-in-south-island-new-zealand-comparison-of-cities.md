## 1. Living in Queenstown: advantages, costs, and the accommodation problem

Queenstown is a dreamy city with a vibrant and touristy atmosphere. Surrounded by the majestic Southern Alps and situated on the shores of crystal-clear Lake Wakatipu. The city shines just as much in winter as it does in summer, with its street artists, food stalls, and breathtaking views from the main pedestrian square facing the lake. Street performers, food stalls, and music accompany the stroll along the shore. I also noted people doing slackline and exercising at the entrance plaza, which, in my opinion, added a plus to living: a relaxed and social atmosphere.

The city center is teeming with restaurants and hotels where you can enjoy delicious, varied, and international cuisine. The price of dishes starts at $20 NZD. I discovered authentic Pad Thai and tried an Argentine ice cream shop overlooking the lake; both moments were quite memorable. On the other hand, for those who want to work, the job market is quite extensive in relation to the service sector.

Chatting fact with fellow travelers: It's very clear that in June there is a trend to move towards ski resorts to work during the winter season, it's necessary to apply for jobs and book accommodations in advance.

### Pros and cons that we observed and other people told us:

* It's essential to book a place with advance notice: The stay can be an aspect to mediate with intensity for those living in Queenstown and without employment with *accommodation*. This is mainly due to two aspects, there aren't many affordable and functional Holiday Parks near by. Other travelers have also told us that it's necessary to get a room with advance notice because there's a lot of demand during the winter season and costs tend to be a bit higher.
* Cons: It's more expensive and quite small, with many inclines on its central streets.
* Pros: It's very beautiful and has an interesting level of social activity.
* Pro 2: There is direct transportation to Milford Sound, which I consider one of the must-see places in New Zealand.
* It's considered the world capital of adventure sports and extremes.

Travel tip: if you're interested in working at a ski resort, keep in mind that although they may open in June, **job searches and contracts tend to close in March or April**.

### 2. Dunedin: Victorian Architecture and Authentic Local Life

This city known as "Daniden" has quite a lot of local and authentic charm. The truth is that it started giving us good vibes even before we visited: in Roxburgh, we met a couple from Dunedin who were camping by a beautiful lake. They were cheerful, friendly, and laid-back, talking very well about their city which sparked our curiosity to get to know it.

When visiting it, we discovered its Victorian architecture of dark bricks, specialty coffee shops street art and independent galleries. I understand that it's a city where people really live their day-to-day lives. Not being as focused on mass tourism, restaurants are more take-away style, focused on the local routine, which makes it a daily option more affordable compared to Queenstown; no doubt an interesting possibility.

## 4. My Final Decision: Living in Christchurch as a Working Holiday 2026

Christchurch is a city in reconstruction with history, community, and spectacular views. The Port Hills hills can be seen from the city and its *trekking* trails are just twenty minutes away, as well as two different beaches within a short hour. The impressive Southern Alps and the incredible **Arthur Pass (you can delve deeper by reading our article)** or some ski resorts are only two hours away by car. From my point of view, this city is the perfect balance between urban social life and constant escapes to nature.

The city is currently in the process of reconstruction after the major earthquake of 2010. Therefore, its infrastructure is modern and the city's layout is comfortable. It is a city that is currently growing, featuring universities, developing edification, an extensive gastronomic proposal, and an increasing population. My local friend commented that many of his acquaintances are moving to this city in search of new opportunities. From my perspective, although one must focus on job searching and persistence, I believe it's a big enough city to offer opportunities for everyone.

**Advantages:** Highlight its modern infrastructure, significant development in the services sector, and wide variety of accommodations available

**Disadvantages:** it's an area of high seismic activity, although it's very organized regarding its safety protocols and you can definitely check with the corresponding apps without a doubt it's a factor to consider. On the other hand, sometimes the lack of travelers and residents can play against you when looking for work (to be honest, I was lucky and found work in 2 weeks, but I know people who took 2 months speaking perfect English).
